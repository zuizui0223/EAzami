# Moreyra et al. 2025 correction — Japanese nuclear backbone

Date: 2026-08-09

## Why this matters

The initial EAzami scaffold treated the Japanese nuclear phylogeny as a large unresolved gap. That is too pessimistic.

Moreyra et al. (2025), *A thorny tale: The origin and diversification of Cirsium (Compositae)*, used target-capture data from 350 nuclear loci for 299 plants / 251 taxa and explicitly included 38 Japanese Cirsium species, 30 of them endemic. The paper recovered all but two sampled Japanese species in a clade interpreted as a rapid Pleistocene radiation following a single dispersal to Japan.

Raw reads are deposited under NCBI BioProject PRJNA957074; sample-level accessions and voucher information are in the paper's supplementary Table S1.

## Consequence for EAzami

RAD-seq should not be justified simply as 'building the first nuclear phylogeny of Japanese Cirsium'. A substantial Japanese nuclear backbone already exists.

The higher-value RAD-seq objectives are now:

1. add Japanese taxa absent from the 38-species target-capture sample, especially flower-colour-transition-critical taxa;
2. add population replication within species represented by only one target-capture sample;
3. resolve within-species white/coloured polymorphism and geographic structure;
4. test introgression and reticulation around candidate colour transitions;
5. connect the Japanese radiation to under-sampled Chinese / Ryukyu / Taiwanese bridge lineages using a shared SNP framework.

## Priority rule update

A Japanese taxon is Tier A only if at least one of the following is true:

- its placement changes the inferred number or direction of white/coloured transitions;
- it contains natural white/coloured polymorphism;
- it is missing from Moreyra et al. 2025 and lies near a transition-critical branch;
- population-level data are required to distinguish regain from ancestral polymorphism or introgression.

A uniformly coloured Japanese species already strongly represented in Moreyra et al. 2025 is a low RAD-seq priority unless it is needed as a flanking control.

## Immediate white-polymorphism candidates in Japan

### Cirsium pendulum

National Museum of Nature and Science records a white-flowered form, Shirobana-taka-azami, within C. pendulum. Noda City also documents naturally occurring white-flowered populations in riverine habitats and notes phenological/ecological differences from ordinary purple-flowered C. pendulum. This is therefore a high-value within-species colour-polymorphism system, potentially comparable to Taiwanese var. takaoense.

Key questions:

- Is the white form monophyletic / geographically structured or repeatedly arising?
- Does white colour share the same anthocyanin regulatory mechanism as Taiwanese and Ryukyu white lineages?
- Are phenology differences genetically linked to the colour locus or environmentally correlated?

### Cirsium sieboldii

The National Museum of Nature and Science explicitly records a white-flowered form, Shirobana-kiseru-azami, within C. sieboldii. This provides a second Japanese within-species white-polymorphism candidate. It should be screened for occurrence frequency and nuclear-backbone coverage before committing to dense RAD-seq sampling.

## Next verification task
