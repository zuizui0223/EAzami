# Korea expansion update — 2026-08-10

## Why Korea matters
Korea is not just a geographic bridge between Japan and continental East Asia. Existing taxonomic names reveal several historical white-flower forms within otherwise coloured *Cirsium* species. This provides a new set of candidate repeated anthocyanin-loss systems independent of the Taiwan and Ryukyu examples.

## Korean taxa audited
The Korean *Cirsium* set currently includes *C. japonicum* (including var. *spinissimum*), *C. schantarense*, *C. rhinoceros*, *C. nipponicum*, *C. setidens*, *C. vlassovianum*, *C. lineare*, and *C. pendulum*. Korean cytological work shows substantial chromosome-number diversity: mostly diploids, one tetraploid (*C. nipponicum*, 2n=68), aneuploid series in *C. setidens* and *C. vlassovianum*, and B chromosomes in some Onotrope taxa. This confirms that ploidy-aware sampling must remain part of the design.

## Newly identified white-form candidates
### *Cirsium setidens*
- normal flower colour: purple in Korean National Arboretum-derived records;
- historical white-form name: *C. setidens* f. *albiflorum* Y.N.Lee;
- Korea endemic;
- cytology: 2n=30/31/32 around x=16.

If extant white populations are verified, this becomes a particularly clean within-species replicate because the species is geographically restricted and the coloured state is independently documented.

### *Cirsium rhinoceros*
- normal flower colour: purple;
- historical white-form names include f. *albiflorum*;
- Korean endemic (Jeju/Hallasan context);
- cytology: 2n=32 in the 2021 study.

This may be an even cleaner endemic replicate than broad transregional systems because geographic population structure is more limited. It should be promoted only after a living or voucher-backed white morph is verified.

### *Cirsium schantarense*
- accepted species distributed from the Russian Far East/Manchuria to Korea;
- historical Korean white-form name: f. *albiflorum* Y.N.Lee;
- cytology: predominantly 2n=34 with B chromosomes reported;
- older ITS data exist but modern dense nuclear placement remains to be verified.

This is valuable as both a repeated-white candidate and a Korea–Russian-Far-East bridge system.

### *Cirsium vlassovianum*
- broad continental distribution from Mongolia/China to the Russian Far East and Korea;
- historical white-form name: f. *album*;
- cytology: 2n=30/31/32 aneuploid series.

If the Korean white morph is real and extant, this system is useful for testing whether white alleles are young/local or old standing variants distributed across continental populations.

## Existing high-priority bridge
### *Cirsium pendulum*
Korean populations are diploid 2n=34 and the species spans Korea, Japan, China, Mongolia and the Russian Far East. Because a stable Japanese white form is already documented, Korean populations are now a key continental coloured bridge. The next test is whether white morphs also occur outside Japan.

## Current interpretation
The Korea audit increases the number of plausible independent white-flower systems substantially. However, nomenclatural white-form names are evidence of historical recognition, not proof of extant natural populations. Therefore these taxa remain `A2_screen` until at least one of the following is verified:
1. a living natural white-flower population;
2. a georeferenced herbarium voucher clearly showing/recording white corollas;
3. repeated independent observations within the same taxon.

Once verified, *C. setidens* and *C. rhinoceros* should be considered especially high-value mechanistic replicates because they are Korean endemics, while *C. schantarense* and *C. vlassovianum* are better for transregional population-history tests.

## Consequence for Chapter 2
The working macro-hypothesis is strengthened:

> Repeated suppression/loss of floral anthocyanin has evolved multiple times across East Asian *Cirsium*, at both species and within-species levels.

The regain hypothesis remains narrower:
- strongest current candidate: bluish-purple *takaoense*;
- not preferred in Arenicola under current sister-clade context;
- not yet identified in the Korean taxa without population-level history.
