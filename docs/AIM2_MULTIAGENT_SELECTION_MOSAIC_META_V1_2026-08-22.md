# Aim 2 multi-agent selection-mosaic meta-synthesis v1 — 2026-08-22

## Question

After the pollinator-selection leverage analysis, one major alternative remains:

> Is there one selective agent that usually dominates floral/reproductive trait evolution, or does the relative contribution of mutualists and antagonists switch among ecological contexts?

This analysis targets HGA3, the assurance-dependent selection-mosaic hypothesis.

## Strict inclusion rule

The primary inference uses only studies that:

1. experimentally manipulated pollination;
2. experimentally manipulated an antagonist or antagonist damage;
3. quantified selection on reproductive/floral traits on a common within-study fitness scale;
4. can distinguish the two agents' contributions without treating repeated populations/years as independent articles.

Five independent programmes / five taxa meet this gate:

- *Gymnadenia conopsea* — Sletvold, Moritz & Ågren 2015, DOI `10.1890/14-0119.1`;
- *Primula farinosa* — Ågren et al. 2013, DOI `10.1073/pnas.1301421110`;
- *Lythrum salicaria* — Austen & Weis 2017, DOI `10.1093/aob/mcx026`;
- *Trifolium repens* — Santangelo et al. 2019, DOI `10.1111/jeb.13392`;
- *Fragaria vesca* — Egan et al. 2021, DOI `10.1002/evl3.262`.

Lower-directness evidence from *Erysimum mediohispanicum*, *Lobelia cardinalis* and *Brassica rapa* is retained as sensitivity evidence. Long-term *Primula farinosa* follow-up is clustered with the 2013 programme rather than counted as a new independent system.

## Result 1 — fixed agent dominance is falsified

The five strict programmes do not share one dominant selective agent:

- pollinator-dominant: **1/5** (*Fragaria*);
- antagonist-dominant: **2/5** (*Lythrum*, *Trifolium*);
- similar / trait-dependent / no fixed dominance: **2/5** (*Gymnadenia*, *Primula*).

This heterogeneity is not created by one outlier: after removing any one programme, at least **two dominance categories** remain.

Therefore neither of the following is defensible as a general rule:

- `pollinators are the dominant selective agent`;
- `antagonists are the dominant selective agent`.

This result is directly relevant to the focal *Cirsium* question: the pooled reproductive-herbivory RR=2.674 cannot be converted into a prior that antagonist-facing traits must be under stronger selection than pollinator-facing traits.

## Result 2 — nonadditivity is common but not universal

Three of five strict programmes show explicit context dependence / diffuse or mosaic selection:

- *Primula farinosa*: relative pollinator and grazer strengths differ among populations and explain morph fitness/evolution;
- *Trifolium repens*: defence state modifies herbivore-mediated selection and some pollinator effects;
- *Fragaria vesca*: every detected pollinator/herbivore selection regime was diffuse, and inflorescence density experienced conflicting selection.

Two programmes show an important counterexample:

- *Gymnadenia conopsea*: mutualist and antagonist selection was strong but statistically additive; their effects conflicted on phenology and reinforced on spur length;
- *Lythrum salicaria*: simulated herbivory changed direct selection, but did not alter pollen limitation or pollinator-mediated selection.

Thus both universal models are rejected:

- `all multi-agent selection is additive` — false;
- `all multi-agent selection is nonadditive` — false.

A selection mosaic does not require a statistical pollinator × antagonist interaction in every population. Additive opposing gradients can still generate spatial or temporal mosaics when the two interaction intensities vary.

## Sensitivity evidence

The lower-directness set strengthens, rather than removes, the heterogeneity:

- *Erysimum mediohispanicum*: ungulate herbivory suppressed floral pollinator-mediated selection;
- *Lobelia cardinalis*: pollinators imposed stronger selection than herbivores, but herbivores were analysed through path models rather than experimentally manipulated;
- *Brassica rapa*: a dual-role cabbage butterfly produced significant nonadditive selection with bumblebees; phenylacetaldehyde selection was `0.20 ± 0.08` below additive expectation and 1-butene-4-isothiocyanate was `0.19 ± 0.08` above it;
- long-term *Primula farinosa*: grazer exclusion strengthened opposing pollinator- and seed-predator-mediated selection as vegetation context changed.

## What this validates

The meta-analysis now supports the **general architecture** of HGA3:

> relative mutualist- versus antagonist-mediated selection is a context-dependent mosaic rather than a fixed hierarchy.

But it does **not** identify the focal *Cirsium* state. The same *Cirsium* population can only be classified after mutualist and antagonist marginal fitness effects are measured on the same seed-fitness scale.

## Integration with the selection-leverage result

The previous restricted gradient meta-analysis showed that:

- broad functional class did not produce a robust universal hierarchy of `|Δβpoll|`;
- increasing pollen limitation in *Lobelia siphilitica* did not strengthen all traits uniformly;
- across-species pollen limitation can increase opportunity for selection, but within-species trait-specific selection depends strongly on local trait→pollination function.

Together, the two meta-analyses imply:

`interaction intensity / assurance`

× `local trait-to-interaction leverage`

× `fitness transmission`

→ realised trait-specific selection.

The multiplication signs are a conceptual factorisation, not an estimated universal equation.

## Focal Cirsium discriminating test

The existing factorial ledger is already aligned with this conclusion:

`ambient vs pollen supplemented`

×

`ambient vs post-anthesis antagonist protected`

with final `filled_achenes / seed mass` measured on the same focal heads.

The new requirement from the meta-analysis is to estimate the local leverage terms on those same populations:

- trait → effective pollinator contact / pollen delivery;
- trait → antagonist access / damage;
- interaction difference → seed fitness.

For orientation, preserve early/mid/late time windows because an orientation can have strong functional leverage through timing, head temperature, wetting or pollen viability even if all-day visits are unchanged.

## Claim boundary

This is a study-program-level falsification meta-synthesis. The included studies use different trait sets and selection estimands, so they are not forced into a pooled common β. The validated conclusion is heterogeneity of agent dominance and ecological context dependence, not a universal effect-size ratio. The focal *Cirsium* selection mosaic remains an empirical prediction until the same-population factorial is run.
