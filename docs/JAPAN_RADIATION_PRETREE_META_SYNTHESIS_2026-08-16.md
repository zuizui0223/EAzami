# Japanese Cirsium pre-tree meta synthesis — 2026-08-16

## Current answer

Existing public and authority-backed data now go beyond the statement that Japanese *Cirsium* underwent a rapid radiation.

Before the accepted 294/296 branch-length nuclear topology is available, the data already show three important constraints:

1. **the dominant Japanese radiation contains large present-day capitulum disparity internally;**
2. **it also spans large present-day environmental-position differences internally;**
3. **neither broad current climate distance nor ploidy class provides a simple one-axis explanation for the observed capitulum differences.**

This does not prove adaptive radiation. It changes which adaptive-radiation models remain worth testing.

## 1. Radiation success is extremely asymmetric

The frozen Japanese-origin meta-analysis places 36 of 38 sampled Japanese paper taxon concepts in one dominant radiation. *C. lineare* is a replicated phylogenetic exception; *C. dipsacolepis* is the current second secondary-arrival candidate.

Thus the first macroevolutionary fact is not simply repeated colonization but **strongly asymmetric diversification success among colonization histories**.

The 36:1:1 occupancy under the current three-history point hypothesis is descriptive, not an age-corrected diversification-rate estimate.

## 2. Colonization separation is not a simple phenotype-distance axis

Nine taxa currently have enough detector-positive public-image records for the frozen seven-axis non-circular trait comparison.

With *C. lineare* as the replicated secondary-history comparator:

- its distance to the dominant-radiation trait centroid is 4.842;
- *C. sieboldii*, inside the dominant radiation, has a larger leave-one-out displacement of 8.103;
- the largest within-dominant trait distance (6.751) exceeds the largest observed *lineare*→dominant distance (6.275).

Therefore deeper/separate colonization history is not a monotonic proxy for current capitulum phenotype distance in this subset.

## 3. Colonization separation is not a simple environmental-distance axis either

Using the same nine taxa and four Azami CHELSA species-median descriptors:

- *C. lineare* is 1.188 standardized units from the dominant-radiation environmental centroid;
- *C. pendulum*, inside the dominant radiation, has a leave-one-out displacement of 6.358;
- the largest within-dominant environmental distance (5.623) exceeds the largest observed *lineare*→dominant distance (3.719).

This is a current environmental-position screen, not a full niche analysis. *C. lineare* has only three balanced Azami observations, so the final ecological comparison requires a dedicated full-occurrence niche layer.

## 4. Broad current climate distance does not track capitulum distance in the current subset

Across the same nine taxa:

- seven-axis capitulum distance × four-axis CHELSA distance: Spearman rho = -0.215;
- taxon-label permutation P for positive coupling = 0.732;
- two-sided permutation P = 0.492;
- all leave-one-taxon-out correlations remain negative.

Module-specific screens sharpen this result:

- orientation: rho ≈ -0.012;
- visible colour (L* + chroma): rho ≈ -0.003;
- outline shape: rho ≈ -0.281.

No module shows positive broad-current-climate distance coupling. The shape result stays negative under every leave-one-taxon-out comparison, but its permutation test is not significant.

This does **not** mean the traits are non-adaptive. It means a simple model in which broad CHELSA climatic separation directly predicts capitulum separation is not supported by this small current dataset. Pollinators, antagonists, microclimate, habitat structure, unmeasured environmental axes and historical/genetic mechanisms remain live.

## 5. Ploidy does not supply a simple morphology syndrome

A conservative source-backed Japan-38 cytotype panel currently contains eight dominant-radiation concepts:

- 2x: 5;
- 4x: 2;
- 6x: 1.

Among non-conflicted orientation states:

- upward/ascending capitula occur in 2x, 4x and 6x taxa;
- diploid dominant-radiation taxa include both upward and downward/nodding capitula.

Therefore ploidy cannot be treated as a deterministic explanation for head orientation. Any role for polyploidy in evolvability must instead be tested through cytotype transitions, homeolog ancestry, gene flow, genomic novelty or diversification association.

## 6. Capitulum states are combinatorial rather than one syndrome per history

The current authority-backed seed includes 20 dominant-radiation concepts and the two secondary-history comparators.

Within the dominant radiation, source-backed states include both upward and downward/nodding capitula and both sticky and non-sticky involucres. Four orientation × stickiness combinations are already represented.

The two secondary-history comparators are both upward/erect, yet differ in involucre stickiness: *C. lineare* is sticky in the current authority seed whereas *C. dipsacolepis* is non-sticky.

Thus even the secondary histories do not define one shared capitulum syndrome.

## 7. The simplest explanations already weakened

Before the tree, the current evidence weakens four simple models:

- `separate/deeper history → greater current phenotype distance`;
- `greater broad current climate distance → greater capitulum distance`;
- `one ploidy class → one head orientation`;
- `one colonization history → one capitulum syndrome`.

None of these eliminations demonstrates the alternative mechanism. They narrow the problem toward **modular and potentially rapidly reusable trait variation**.

## 8. What this does to HMM3/HMM6

### HMM3 — radiation success / evolvability

Strengthened as a question, not yet demonstrated.

The dominant radiation is species-rich and already contains substantial trait/environment disparity that does not simply mirror colonization history. The branch-length tree must now determine whether that disparity accumulated unusually rapidly rather than merely being large in current state space.

### HMM6 — cross-scale trait decoupling

Strengthened.

Azami had already shown that visible within-species dispersion does not collapse onto one common climate-association axis. The Japanese pre-tree analysis now adds that **among-taxon capitulum distance also does not show a simple positive relation with broad current climate distance**, and the relationship differs by module.

## 9. Next tests that still require no new biological sample

Once the accepted nuclear topology ensemble is available:

1. branch-scaled trait-rate/disparity analyses across the dominant radiation and secondary histories;
2. ancestral/state-transition analysis for colour, orientation and authority-backed involucral states;
3. full-occurrence niche-space comparison rather than current species-median environment positions;
4. gene-tree concordance/discordance and reduced-network metrics;
5. source-backed cytotype/genome-size transition mapping;
6. age- and sampling-corrected radiation-success comparisons.

New broad mainland sampling remains lower priority until these public-data tests identify the exact branch or mechanism that remains non-identifiable.

## 10. What still cannot be solved without new biological data

Population-level data remain essential for:

- morph↔genotype linkage beyond var. *takaoense*;
- standing ancestral variation versus introgression;
- mixed cytotypes and ploidy-aware local ancestry;
- genotype/homeolog histories in polyploids;
- trait → pollinator/antagonist/abiotic interaction → fitness;
- genotype → floral expression → pigment → phenotype mechanisms.

These are the explicit reasons for the previously frozen Phase-A RAD/plastid/flow-cytometry panel and later functional experiments.

## Current wording boundary

Supported wording:

> Japanese *Cirsium* contains a rapid, strongly asymmetric radiation whose current capitulum and environmental disparity is large within the dominant young lineage and is not simply predicted by colonization-history separation, broad climate distance or ploidy class.

Still hypothesis-level:

> The radiation succeeded because reticulation, standing variation, polyploidy and regulatory modularity increased evolvability.

Not yet supported:

> Japanese *Cirsium* is a demonstrated adaptive radiation, or a particular capitulum trait caused its diversification.
