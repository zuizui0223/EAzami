# FDT1 bract/phyllary defence primary-manipulation audit (2026-08-26)

## Disposition

The bounded primary-source search recovered **four independent direct experiments** outside the excluded systems. Two are especially consequential for the defensive-envelope module:

1. *Centaurea solstitialis* (Cardueae): removing the spines borne by involucral bracts increased visits by illegitimate lepidopteran flower visitors and reduced the percentage of filled seeds. This is the closest external analogue to a focal *Cirsium* phyllary/spine experiment because it directly manipulates a Cardueae capitulum defence and follows the same plants to a seed endpoint.
2. *Monotropsis odorata*: removing persistent dry bracts increased reproductive herbivory in both study years and reduced the mature-fruit ratio in one of two years. This closes a direct `bract package -> reproductive damage -> mature fruit` path, but the treatment changed both visual crypsis and physical covering.

Two additional experiments isolate access mechanisms without a final seed/fruit endpoint:

3. *Taraxacum japonicum* × *officinale*: cutting recurved outer phyllaries approximately halved the time slugs spent negotiating the capitulum barrier, making the cut hybrid resemble a native taxon with erect phyllaries.
4. *Chrysothemis friedrichsthaliana*: draining a water-filled fused-sepal envelope more than doubled the odds of specialist-moth infestation and floral sterilization.

The evidence state therefore changes from `no direct external reproductive-envelope manipulation` to `direct external causal support at access/damage and, in two systems, fruit/seed endpoints`. It does **not** establish that Azami image-derived spine-like geometry is a botanical defence trait, that the same mechanism operates in *Cirsium*, or that heterogeneous envelope effects can be pooled into one common effect.

## Admission rule

A study had to:

- be a primary experimental report;
- directly remove, cut, drain, open or otherwise manipulate a floral/reproductive bract, phyllary, spine-bearing involucre or comparable enclosing structure;
- quantify antagonist access, visitation, infestation or damage to reproductive tissues;
- be independent of *Pedicularis rex* (DOI `10.1093/aobpla/plv019`) and the registered Aquilegia, Bejaria, Datura, Passiflora, Erica and *Cirsium discolor* systems;
- not rely solely on an observational trait correlation; and
- retain the original experimental unit, clustering and endpoint rather than treating flowers, visits or seeds as automatically independent.

No values were reconstructed from P-values, test statistics or secondary summaries. Values reported only graphically are identified as digitizable but were not visually converted into effect sizes here.

## Study 1 — *Centaurea solstitialis*: removal of involucral-bract spines

### Identity and primary source

Agrawal, A. A., Rudgers, J. A., Botsford, L. W., Cutler, D., Gorin, J. B., Lundquist, C. J., Spitzer, B. W. & Swann, A. L. (2000). Benefits and constraints on plant defense against herbivores: spines influence the legitimate and illegitimate flower visitors of yellow star thistle, *Centaurea solstitialis* L. (Asteraceae). *The Southwestern Naturalist* 45(1):1–5. DOI: [10.2307/3672545](https://doi.org/10.2307/3672545). [Primary article PDF on the first author's publication site](https://live-agrawal.pantheonsite.io/pdfs/2000/starthistle-2000.pdf); [author publication index](https://agrawal.eeb.cornell.edu/publications-1/).

### Exact manipulation and experimental unit

- Site and season: a heavily disturbed site near Orchard Park, University of California at Davis, October–November 1997.
- Biological replicate: 15 individual plants.
- Paired design: each plant was visually divided in half. The authors randomly selected one half and removed all mature spines with small scissors. The other half retained its spines.
- Treatment maintenance: spines were trimmed throughout the remaining flowering season to maintain paired opened flowers on spined and spineless sides of each plant.
- The spines were described as mature dead tissue. The authors therefore considered induction from cutting unlikely, but there was no separate sham-cut treatment.
- Visitor observations were made on 12 occasions for approximately one hour each (`12 h` total). Visits and visit durations were aggregated by plant/visitor identity for the reported paired tests.
- Seed endpoint: after senescence, the authors harvested two similarly aged heads per plant, one from each treatment side, dissected them and counted filled and unfilled seeds. The plant, not the head or individual seed, is the paired biological replicate.

### Exact reported outcomes

| endpoint | exact primary result | inference-safe use |
|---|---|---|
| illegitimate lepidopteran visits | `n = 26 visits`; more visits to spineless heads; `Z = 2.138`, `P = 0.032` | direct causal direction for spine presence reducing illegitimate-flower-visitor access |
| legitimate bee/fly visits | `n = 54 visits`; no treatment difference; `Z = 0.815`, `P = 0.415` | visitation-incidence null retained |
| lepidopteran visit duration | no treatment difference; Friedman statistic `0.421`, `df = 1`, `P = 0.516` | duration null retained separately from visitation incidence |
| legitimate bee/fly visit duration | visits were more than `22%` longer on spineless heads; Friedman statistic `4.939`, `df = 1`, `P = 0.026` | potential pollinator-handling cost of the intact defence, not a pollination-fitness effect by itself |
| filled-seed percentage | spineless heads had a `22%` reduction relative to spined controls; `Z = 2.542`, `P = 0.011` | direct final-seed direction for the paired spine-removal experiment |

The article plots arm means with standard errors in Fig. 4, but does not print their exact numerical values or the within-plant paired covariance. The exact `22%` relative reduction is reported in the primary abstract; it is not an estimate inferred from the P-value.

### Quantitative readiness and claim ceiling

This study is admissible as a **paired causal calibration with a final seed endpoint**, but it is not yet safe for inverse-variance pooling. Digitizing Fig. 4 would recover marginal arm means and standard errors, not the missing within-plant covariance. The Wilcoxon statistic must not be transformed into a sampling variance for a mean ratio by assumption. Author-level paired head data, a paired mean/SE of the difference, or an estimand that validly uses the rank statistic would be required.

The experiment supports `involucral-bract spines -> fewer illegitimate lepidopteran visits -> higher filled-seed proportion` in *C. solstitialis*. It does not measure florivore or seed-predator damage directly, and it does not experimentally prove that the visitor change mediated the seed response. It also does not show a net effect in *Cirsium* or validate any 2-D Azami spine proxy.

## Study 2 — *Taraxacum* hybrid: recurved outer-phyllary removal

### Identity and primary sources

Wu, F.-Y. & Yahara, T. (2017). Recurved *Taraxacum* phyllaries function as a floral defense: experimental evidence and its implication for *Taraxacum* evolutionary history. *Ecological Research* 32:313–329. DOI: [10.1007/s11284-017-1444-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11284-017-1444-5). [Publisher article record and primary abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11284-017-1444-5); [full primary doctoral manuscript in the Kyushu University repository](https://api.lib.kyushu-u.ac.jp/opac_download_md/1806849/sls0185.pdf), repository DOI [10.15017/1806849](https://doi.org/10.15017/1806849).

### Exact manipulation and experimental unit

- System: an agamospermous *T. japonicum* × *officinale* hybrid with recurved outer phyllaries, native *T. japonicum* with erect outer phyllaries, and the naturalized slug *Lehmannia valentiana*.
- Direct experiment: recurved outer phyllaries were cut from hybrid capitula. Intact hybrid capitula were the treatment control; native *T. japonicum* supplied a nonmanipulated erect-phyllary comparator.
- Repeated unit: the same 14 slugs were sequentially placed on native, cut-hybrid and intact-hybrid stems and video-recorded. The treatment order was fixed in that sequence.
- Plant reuse: each daily experiment used one native and two hybrid plants; capitula were reused within the day and removed after the daily experiment. The manuscript does not supply a plant-level treatment ledger or a slug-by-plant raw table.
- Endpoint: time spent negotiating the capitulum/phyllary segment and whether a slug retreated. No fruit or seed output was measured.

### Exact reported outcome

One of 14 slugs retreated on contacting an intact hybrid's recurved phyllaries. For the remaining 13 slugs, the primary manuscript reports:

| treatment | time on the capitulum/phyllary segment |
|---|---:|
| intact hybrid, recurved phyllaries | `84.64 ± 44.68 s` |
| hybrid, recurved outer phyllaries cut | `41.21 ± 28.63 s` |
| native *T. japonicum*, erect phyllaries | `32.07 ± 17.10 s` |

The authors report an overall `F(2,13) = 12.82`, `P < 0.05`; intact versus cut hybrid `P < 0.001`; and intact hybrid versus native `P < 0.001`. Cut hybrid and native did not differ. The accessible manuscript does not clearly define whether the `±` values are standard deviations or standard errors, so no sampling variance is assigned here.

A separate species-comparison exposure assay found damage to all eight native plants and none of eight hybrid plants across five assay dates. That contrast is not the direct removal experiment and is confounded by other taxon differences, including inner-phyllary length; it must not be used as a phyllary-removal damage effect.

### Quantitative readiness and claim ceiling

The direct treatment supports a physical-access mechanism: recurved outer phyllaries delayed slug movement onto the capitulum, and cutting them removed that delay. It is not meta-ready because the repeated-slug design, within-day plant reuse, fixed treatment order, missing plant ledger, undefined dispersion scale and unusual reported ANOVA degrees of freedom prevent reconstruction of an independent sampling variance.

The study does not close `phyllary -> florivory damage -> seed fitness`, and its geographic survey does not by itself demonstrate coevolution or repeated adaptive origins. The defensible use is a direction-only phyllary-access calibration and a concrete design warning for Chapter 2 experiments: randomize treatment order, retain slug and plant identifiers, and follow the manipulated capitulum to filled seed.

## Study 3 — *Monotropsis odorata*: removal of a persistent dry-bract envelope

### Identity and primary source

Klooster, M. R., Clark, D. L. & Culley, T. M. (2009). Cryptic bracts facilitate herbivore avoidance in the mycoheterotrophic plant *Monotropsis odorata* (Ericaceae). *American Journal of Botany* 96:2197–2205. DOI: [10.3732/ajb.0900124](https://doi.org/10.3732/ajb.0900124). [Free primary full text from the journal](https://bsapubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.3732/ajb.0900124).

### Exact manipulation and experimental unit

- Plants were defined as isolated clumps of reproductive stems at least 1 m from the next clump.
- In early March 2006 and 2007, plants were randomly assigned to unmanipulated control or bract removal.
- Sample size per treatment was 45 plants in 2006 and 25 plants in 2007.
- For the experimental plants, all dry, sterile bracts were removed from every reproductive stem with forceps and surgical scissors, exposing flowers and stems.
- There was no sham-removal group and no proposed painted-bract control, so the treatment simultaneously removed physical covering and leaf-litter-like visual crypsis.
- Flowers were classified at harvest as maturing fruit, aborted ovary/fruit, or damaged by reproductive herbivory. Stem values were averaged to a plant-level ratio before treatment comparison.

### Exact reported outcomes

| year | control herbivory | bracts removed | test | control mature fruit | bracts removed | test |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---|
| 2006 | `9%` | `36%` | `Z = 4.49`, `P < 0.0001` | `55%` | `35%` | `Z = 2.89`, `P = 0.0038` |
| 2007 | `17%` | `37%` | `Z = -2.49`, `P = 0.0128` | `45%` | `38%` | `Z = 0.41`, `P = 0.682` |

The article's herbivory-free sensitivity analysis removed all stems with any herbivory. Mature-fruit ratios then did not differ between control and removal plants in 2006 (`56%` versus `49%`, `P = 0.4354`) or 2007 (`56%` versus `52%`, `P = 0.9389`). Fruit-abortion ratios also did not differ by treatment. These checks make a simple cutting/desiccation explanation less likely, but they are a post-treatment subset and are not a formal causal mediation analysis.

### Quantitative readiness and claim ceiling

The plant-level means, sample sizes and nonparametric tests are exact, while the numerical standard errors are plotted but not printed. Do not derive a variance from the reported P-values. Figure digitization could recover marginal SEs, but raw plant-level ratios would be preferable for a bounded outcome model.

This study supports `persistent dry-bract package -> lower reproductive herbivory -> preserved mature-fruit ratio` in one system, with the fruit response replicated directionally but not significantly in the second year. Reflectance measurements make crypsis plausible, yet the absent painted-bract control means optical camouflage cannot be isolated from physical obstruction or other bract functions. It is an envelope-package calibration, not a pure mechanical-spine effect.

## Study 4 — *Chrysothemis friedrichsthaliana*: drained water-calyx barrier

### Identity and primary source

Carlson, J. E. & Harms, K. E. (2007). The benefits of bathing buds: water calyces protect flowers from a microlepidopteran herbivore. *Biology Letters*. DOI: [10.1098/rsbl.2007.0095](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2007.0095). [Primary article PDF in the Smithsonian repository](https://repository.si.edu/bitstreams/ace2af01-211b-4a48-a331-93b250ebd7aa/download).

### Exact manipulation and experimental unit

- Early buds were randomly assigned to an intact water calyx, a drained calyx, or a drained-and-refilled handling control, with at least 80 buds initially assigned to each treatment.
- Treatments were repeated daily for up to 21 days or until consumption or anthesis.
- The handling control and intact treatment did not differ for herbivory or abortion (both linear contrasts `t < 0.13`, `P > 0.90`) and were pooled by the authors.
- Final sample sizes were 138 drained and 219 undrained buds.
- The response unit in the reported model was the individual bud/flower, with year as a random effect. The paper says more than 350 plants were monitored but does not report how many experimental buds came from each plant or include plant as a clustering factor.

### Exact reported outcome

Draining more than doubled the odds that a flower carried specialist alucitid larvae and lacked anthers and stigmas at opening: odds ratio `2.18`, 95% CI `1.21–3.92`, `F(1,241) = 6.87`, `P = 0.009`. More than 98% of buds with oviposition scars in both treatments became sterile. The treatment did not change complete consumption by generalist herbivores (`P = 0.88`) or premature bud abortion (`P = 0.27`).

### Quantitative readiness and claim ceiling

The log odds ratio and confidence interval are exactly extractable from the primary paper. However, admission to an independent-effect meta-analysis should remain `pending_host_plant_clustering_resolution`: treating multiple buds from the same unreported host plant as independent would understate uncertainty.

This comparable envelope experiment supports `liquid-filled calyx -> reduced specialist-moth oviposition/access -> reduced floral sterilization`. It does not show generalized herbivore exclusion, and floral sterilization is not a measured final fruit or seed endpoint.

## Context-dependent counterexample retained

Song et al. (2013), *Rheum nobile*, DOI [10.1007/s00442-012-2518-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00442-012-2518-2), directly manipulated showy bracts and found that bracts improved several abiotic, pollination and offspring endpoints while **increasing**, not decreasing, seed predation by larvae of pollinators. The [publisher's primary abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00442-012-2518-2) is sufficient to retain the direction as a counterexample, but not to extract a common enemy-damage effect without the full numerical methods and results. This prevents a universal `bracts = antagonist defence` rule and reinforces the Chapter 2 selection-mosaic framing.

## Bounded search routes and STOP

The search combined `bract removal`, `involucral bract`, `phyllary removal`, `inflorescence spine removal`, `florivory`, `seed predator`, `nectar robbing`, `mature fruit`, `filled seed` and `water calyx`. Candidates were followed from primary reference lists and the recent floral-bract review, then checked against publisher full text, an author-hosted article PDF or an institutional primary manuscript. The already registered systems were excluded before admission.

The search stopped after recovering:

- one direct Cardueae spine-removal experiment with a final filled-seed endpoint;
- one direct phyllary-removal access experiment in Asteraceae;
- one independent bract-removal experiment closing damage to mature fruit;
- one exact comparable-envelope log odds ratio; and
- one opposite-direction bract/seed-predation counterexample.

No additional focal *Cirsium* phyllary/spine manipulation was recovered. The next action is not broader searching. It is source recovery or author-data contact for the paired *Centaurea* outcomes, clarification of host-plant clustering for *Chrysothemis*, and a focal experiment only after direct botanical validation of *Cirsium* phyllary posture, spine length/direction and repeatable within-population variation.

## FDT1 decision

1. Admit *C. solstitialis* as a **direct Cardueae direction-plus-fitness calibration**, with quantitative pooling blocked by missing paired covariance/raw values.
2. Admit *Taraxacum* as a **direction-only physical-access calibration**, not as a damage or fitness effect.
3. Admit *M. odorata* as a **compound bract-envelope damage-to-fruit calibration**, stratified as visual-plus-physical concealment.
4. Retain the *Chrysothemis* log odds ratio as **numerically exact but clustering-pending**.
5. Do not pool visitor incidence, handling time, herbivory ratios, floral sterilization and filled-seed proportions into one effect merely because all involve enclosing structures.
6. Replace any repository wording that says no direct external phyllary/spine manipulation exists. Preserve the stricter statement: **no direct focal *Cirsium* manipulation has yet validated the Azami image proxy or closed antagonist and pollinator pathways to filled achenes**.

For Chapter 2, these studies justify a mechanistic `defensive envelope` row in the trait-by-function matrix and motivate repeated-evolution questions. They do not license a repeated-adaptation claim until the focal morphology, transition history, ecological mediator and reproductive-fitness effect are independently closed.
