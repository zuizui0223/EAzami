# Sinocirsium flower-colour history update

Date: 2026-08-10

## Published phylogenetic scaffold
Chang et al. (2026) resolves subsect. Sinocirsium as follows at the lineage level:

- var. japonicum is the basal lineage;
- Taiwanese varieties form two sister pairs: var. albescens + var. takaoense, and var. australe + var. fukienense;
- all four Taiwanese varieties are reciprocally monophyletic at the lineage level, although internal support within var. takaoense is low and its sample topology varies among analyses.

Flower-colour evidence used here:
- var. japonicum: coloured (red to purple in Flora of China treatment of C. japonicum);
- var. albescens: all white;
- var. takaoense: white and bluish-purple morphs;
- var. australe: bluish purple;
- var. fukienense: bluish-purple to lighter purple, still coded as coloured in the binary analysis.

## Population-aware parsimony result
Using one white and one bluish-purple takaoense population/morph tip under the published macro-topology gives:

### Coloured Sinocirsium root
Minimum = 2 state changes, with two equally parsimonious biological histories:

1. **Repeated-loss history:** 2 C->W losses, 0 W->C regains.
   - white evolves independently in var. albescens and in the white takaoense lineage/population.

2. **Shared-white + regain history:** 1 C->W loss, 1 W->C regain.
   - a white state evolves on the albescens-takaoense ancestral branch, then pigmentation reappears in the bluish-purple takaoense lineage/population.

These histories are indistinguishable by equal-cost parsimony alone.

### White Sinocirsium root
Minimum = 3 W->C gains. This is substantially less economical because the basal var. japonicum and the australe-fukienense lineage are coloured.

## Interpretation
This makes bluish-purple var. takaoense the strongest current **candidate regain** in the project, but not a demonstrated regain. The same observed states are equally compatible with independent white-flower evolution in albescens and white takaoense.

The published study further reports low internal support/topological variation within var. takaoense and reticulate signals among C. japonicum varieties, so ancestral polymorphism and introgression remain serious alternatives.

## What distinguishes the competing histories
### Prediction: shared-white + regain
- white albescens and white takaoense should share an ancestral colour-suppression haplotype or homologous regulatory state;
- bluish-purple takaoense should carry a derived restoration/reactivation change on that background;
- local ancestry around the colour locus should not be better explained by introgression from australe/fukienense.

### Prediction: repeated independent loss
- albescens and white takaoense should have distinct causal mutations/haplotypes despite the same phenotype;
- bluish-purple takaoense should retain the ancestral coloured state rather than show a derived restoration signature.

### Prediction: ancestral polymorphism
- white/coloured haplotypes may predate lineage divergence and show deep coalescence across taxa without a simple sequential loss-regain history.

### Prediction: introgression
- the bluish-purple takaoense colour-associated region should show excess ancestry sharing with coloured australe/fukienense relative to the genomic background.

## Sampling consequence
This strengthens the priority of paired white/bluish-purple takaoense population sampling. The decisive data are population-level nuclear history plus pigment/RNA/genomic mechanism, not another species-level tree.
