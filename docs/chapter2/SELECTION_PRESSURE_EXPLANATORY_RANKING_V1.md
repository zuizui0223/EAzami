# Selection-pressure explanatory ranking for capitulum diversification v1

Status date: 2026-08-30

## Decision

A candidate selective pressure becomes more credible when **independent evidence dimensions converge on the same ecological domain**. The current synthesis therefore does not rank factors by one P value. It records whether each trait × pressure hypothesis is supported by:

1. **R — repeated history:** the phenotype changed repeatedly on the accepted topology ensemble;
2. **T — event placement:** the repeated history has an identifiable relative or absolute temporal geometry;
3. **S — spatial breadth:** the homologous phenotype is sorted along a present-day Azami environmental gradient;
4. **C — current ecological correspondence:** present states occupy different niches after phylogenetic correction in EAzami;
5. **L — independent mechanism prior:** experiments or quantitative synthesis outside the focal comparison support the proposed trait → process link;
6. **P — historical environment:** dated transition windows align with prespecified environmental change or variability under an admissible null;
7. **F — focal function and fitness:** manipulating the trait changes the nominated mechanism and reproductive fitness in ancestry-resolved focal populations.

The machine-readable matrix is `../../data/evidence/chapter2_selection_pressure_triangulation_v1.csv`.

R, T, S, C and L can strengthen a **selection-pressure candidate**. P is required before the factor can be described as historically corresponding to transitions. F is required before the chain approaches an adaptive explanation. No current trait reaches P or F.

## Why no numerical score is used

The seven dimensions are not exchangeable units. A repeated-history result cannot compensate for missing phenotype homology, and ten weak correlations cannot replace one direct trait → fitness experiment. A summed score would also hide whether apparent agreement comes from independent datasets or from reusing the same occurrence and climate layers. We therefore use evidence classes and preserve the exact missing link.

This is a ranking of **current explanatory closure**, not biological importance.

## 1. Orientation × hydric exposure is the strongest current candidate

### Independent evidence that agrees

**Repeated history.** Orientation requires four to six minimum changes across the 1,000-topology bootstrap ensemble, with a median of five. The observed distribution is not explained by one old split.

**Relative timing.** The median exact relative-depth envelope is 0.795–0.994. Minimum histories can include internal and terminal changes, so orientation was reconfigured at more than one lineage scale. Exact calendar dates remain unavailable.

**Spatial breadth.** In Azami, higher annual precipitation amount (BIO12) is associated among taxa with a larger signed head-axis angle relative to image vertical. The direction survives the broad-space screen, all 52 historical-placement sensitivities and all applicable sampling-composition perturbations.

**Current ecological correspondence.** In EAzami, downward/nodding taxa occupy niches with higher precipitation seasonality (BIO15). The sign survives all accepted topologies, all species leave-one-out fits and both independent Taiwan occurrence-source tiers. The threshold-based class changes between `unresolved` and `tendency_supported`, but the biological direction does not.

**Independent mechanism prior.** External manipulations and synthesis support wetting/pollen-performance and presentation pathways as biologically credible, although no direct focal Japanese *Cirsium* manipulation closes the mechanism.

### What the agreement means

BIO12 and BIO15 are different quantities. Their agreement is not replication of one coefficient; it is **hydric-domain convergence across independent facets**:

- Azami asks where phenotypes are sorted along the spatial gradient in mean annual water input;
- EAzami asks whether current evolutionary states occupy niches differing in the within-year unevenness of precipitation.

Because the trait also changed repeatedly, the combined result is more explanatory than either the spatial association or the reconstruction alone. It supports the hypothesis that repeated orientation states may be associated with changing benefits of reproductive exposure and presentation under different rainfall regimes.

### What remains open

P is missing: no admitted dated transition windows have yet been compared with rainfall variability through geological time. F is missing: rain interception, pollen retention/viability, effective pollinator contact and filled-achene production have not been jointly manipulated in ancestry-resolved focal populations.

Therefore the allowed conclusion is:

> **Hydric exposure is the strongest public-data candidate selective-pressure domain for repeated capitulum-orientation diversification.**

It is not yet `rain adaptation`, event-specific environmental causation or adaptive convergence.

## 2. Orientation × thermal regime is informative because it does not align simply

EAzami places downward taxa in lower BIO1 niches, with a stable negative D-minus-U direction. Azami, however, retains a positive within-taxon orientation–BIO1 broad-space association. These estimands differ in both scale and composition.

This blocks a universal statement such as “downward heads are a cold-climate phenotype.” Several explanations remain distinguishable:

- a within-taxon geographic response can differ from among-lineage historical sorting;
- BIO1 may proxy elevation, snow, phenology, precipitation seasonality or pollinator turnover inside East Asia;
- present taxon niche centroids may differ from conditions at transition time;
- the image-axis orientation endpoint may contain scale-specific measurement structure.

The mismatch is therefore a **scale-dependent competing pathway**, not evidence that temperature is irrelevant. A dated and scale-matched analysis is required before direct thermal selection is promoted.

## 3. Colour × radiation is spatially strong but temporally unclosed

Azami shows robust among-taxon sorting of visible corolla chroma along mean shortwave radiation: higher radiation is associated with lower visible CIELAB chroma. An independent colour/UV/pigment literature supplies plausible optical and physiological pathways.

The time axis does not yet close:

- no sequenced-individual W/C/P transition ontology is admitted for Japan38;
- lightness, chroma and hue do not retain corrected two-sided support in the frozen continuous-history family;
- no dated colour-transition window or transition-level ecology is available.

Two explanations remain live. Colour may be sufficiently labile to track local environments and leave weak phylogenetic structure, or the Japanese matched panel may be too sparse and species-tip compressed to recover its history. Current data cannot choose between them.

The negative visible-chroma coefficient also cannot identify anthocyanin concentration, UV absorption or pigment-pathway activity. Colour is therefore a strong **spatial mechanism candidate**, not yet a space–time adaptive explanation.

## 4. Phyllary posture has a strong temporal pattern but no identified driver

Phyllary posture requires exactly three minimum changes across all 1,000 bootstrap topologies. Its relative-depth envelope, 0.695–1.000, is the broadest among the three discrete traits. This allows both a relatively broad-lineage reorganization and terminal reconfiguration.

Several pressure domains are plausible—reproductive-enemy exclusion, wetting protection, mechanical protection and pollinator-access cost—but current evidence cannot rank them. Azami projection, taper and roughness endpoints are not homologous to authority-coded ascending, spreading or recurved posture, and the EAzami climate panel lacks a state-diverse comparison.

The proper class is **history with driver unidentified**. Borrowing the orientation hydric result or calling all involucre geometry defence would manufacture agreement.

## 5. Stickiness shows shallow recurrence but weakens a universal defence story

Stickiness requires exactly five changes across all 1,000 bootstrap topologies and has a narrow, shallow median relative-depth envelope of approximately 0.937–0.954. This is compatible with repeated lineage-local reassembly.

A local biotic-selection mosaic is plausible: reproductive enemies, florivores, associated arthropod communities and secretion costs can vary faster and more locally than broad climate means. But the independent mechanism evidence is not uniformly supportive. A direct *Cirsium discolor* neutralization did not support the generic sticky-defence prediction, while benefits or costs occur in non-equivalent systems.

Thus repeated history does not rescue the generic mechanism. The current result is:

> **Stickiness is recurrent, but a universal enemy-exclusion adaptation is weakened; context-dependent benefit, cost or another function remains unresolved.**

This is a valuable negative triangulation result.

## 6. Pollinator and enemy hypotheses cannot be inferred from climate layers

For orientation and colour, pollinator-mediated functions remain plausible, but no dense and harmonized pollinator-opportunity history is joined to the focal transitions. Visitor counts are also not equivalent to effective pollen transfer or fitness.

For phyllary posture, stickiness and armature, enemy pressure may be more relevant than climatic averages. The absence of a matching CHELSA signal would therefore not refute a biotic driver. Historical pollinator/enemy turnover needs its own data series and null model rather than being smuggled in through temperature or precipitation proxies.

## How factor agreement changes the claim

The following promotion ladder is frozen:

| Evidence combination | Allowed interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| S only | present spatial association |
| R + T only | recurrent history with relative temporal geometry |
| S + L | spatial mechanism candidate |
| R + T + L | recurrent-trait mechanism candidate |
| R + T + S + C in the same domain | space–time selection-pressure candidate |
| previous level + independent L | **multi-axis concordant selection-pressure candidate** |
| previous level + P | historical environmental correspondence candidate |
| previous level + focal F | adaptive explanation becomes testable |

This ladder does not make significance cumulative by arithmetic. Every promotion requires the new evidence type to address a genuinely different link.

## Current conclusion

> **Capitulum diversity is best explained provisionally by trait-specific selection mosaics operating at different spatial and temporal scales. Repeated orientation history, global precipitation-amount sorting, East-Asian precipitation-seasonality correspondence and independent functional evidence converge most strongly on hydric exposure, making it the leading selective-pressure hypothesis. Radiation-associated colour, deeper phyllary reorganization and shallow stickiness recurrence show complementary but incomplete evidence chains. Their differences are not noise to be averaged away; they indicate that different capitulum components may respond to different environmental statistics, selective agents and temporal scales.**

## Next decisive tests

1. Link an accession- or node-audited dated chronogram to the full reconstruction envelope; do not scale event ages from one radiation date.
2. Test orientation transition windows against prespecified hydric-variability histories under a branch-duration/topology/coverage-conditioned null.
3. Complete gravity-referenced orientation manipulation through wetting, pollen performance, effective contact and filled-achene output.
4. Build a population-linked colour transition map before testing historical radiation correspondence or molecular reuse.
5. Calibrate direct phyllary/armature measurements and retain simultaneous enemy benefit and pollinator cost; test stickiness with sham-controlled focal neutralization.

## Claim ceiling

Multi-axis concordance strengthens a candidate selective-pressure explanation. It does not establish ancestral climate, event-specific causation, adaptation, independent origins, convergence or reproductive-fitness benefit.
