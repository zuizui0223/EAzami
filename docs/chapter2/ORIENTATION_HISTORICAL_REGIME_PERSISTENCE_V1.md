# Orientation H4 — historical persistence of the present-niche transition regime

## Question

Does the present-niche U->D regime identified by H1/H2 (`BIO15 up + BIO1 down`) also characterize the sole calendarized U->D origin event on the core-*Nipponocirsium* stem?

No new variable was screened. The existing 94 chronology pairs × four paleolocation regions (376 deterministic scenarios) were matched across BIO15 and BIO1. A scenario matched H4 only when `BIO15 delta > 0` and `BIO1 delta < 0`. Support required >=75% matching chronology scenarios in **each** region.

## Result

Classification: `historical_regime_persistence_not_supported`.

- Overall H4 matches: **99/376 = 26.3%**.
- Taiwan: **20/94 = 21.3%**.
- Ryukyu corridor: **9/94 = 9.6%**.
- Southern Japan: **41/94 = 43.6%**.
- East-Asia core corridor: **29/94 = 30.9%**.
- Only **6/94** chronology pairs matched H4 in all four regions; **14/94** matched in at least three regions.

The central 0.79 -> 0.74 Ma pair mismatched H4 in all four regions: BIO1 decreased as expected, but BIO15 also decreased everywhere.

## Interpretation

The current bidirectional orientation–niche regime should **not** be projected backward as the historical trigger of the core-*Nipponocirsium* U->D event. The result supports a specific **history-present / origin-maintenance decoupling**: present lineage sorting or maintenance can align strongly with a climate regime even when the one bounded origin event does not robustly show the same historical sign pattern.

This does not show that environment was irrelevant at origin, nor does it identify an alternative historical cause. Scenario fractions are sensitivity-grid fractions, not ancestral probabilities.
