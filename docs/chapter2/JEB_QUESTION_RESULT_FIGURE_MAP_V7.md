Status: working scientific map, 2026-09-03

The V7 figure order follows the positive historical-assembly argument rather than the old trigger-first V6 route.

## Figure 1 — Diversity to be assembled within the dominant radiation

**Question:** Is capitulum diversity mainly a contrast among separate colonization histories, or does one dominant young radiation already contain multiple configurations?

**Primary panels**
- A: Japan38 radiation membership summary: 36/38 dominant-radiation concepts plus the two current secondary-history exceptions/candidates.
- B: source-backed dominant-radiation orientation × stickiness configuration matrix.
- C: optional compact supporting inset showing present trait-space distances: `C. lineare` dominant-centroid distance 4.842 versus dominant LOO maximum 8.103 and within-dominant pairwise maximum 6.751.

**Headline:** multiple capitulum configurations and large trait disparity occur inside the dominant radiation.

**Claim ceiling:** sampled-history and current state-space diversity; not diversification rate, adaptation or complete factorial independence.

## Figure 2 — Repeated component histories and unequal evolutionary depth

**Question:** How many changes are required, and do the three component histories occupy the same relative depth?

**Primary panels**
- A: minimum-change distributions across ML + 1,000 UFBoot topologies:
  - orientation ML 6, UFBoot 4–6;
  - phyllary 3/3 invariant;
  - stickiness 5/5 invariant.
- B: topology-only relative lineage-depth envelopes for orientation, phyllary and stickiness.
- C: paired-topology lower-bound ordering from PR #160 after pinned-runtime admission.
- D: compact 0/3 shared-transition-localization summary.

**Headline:** the components repeatedly differentiated, but their minimum histories are temporally stratified and do not repeatedly share one synchronized localization pattern.

**Claim ceiling:** minimum-change and topology-depth history only; not independent origins, rates, genetic modularity or event ages.

## Figure 3 — Present ecological correspondence is scale-partitioned

**Question:** Does orientation show one common environmental response across within-taxon, among-taxon and East-Asian state scales?

**Primary panels**
- BIO12: within +0.00533, q=0.874 versus among +0.30436, q=0.00640 (`among_only`).
- BIO1: within +0.01715, q=0.0349 versus among -0.03024, q=0.836, with negative EAzami downward-minus-upward state contrast.
- BIO15: unsupported Azami within/among coefficients alongside EAzami downward-minus-upward +1.320–1.330 SD and sign-stability annotation.

Prefer a coefficient/scale matrix over a geography-heavy plot.

**Headline:** ecological organization exists, but the supported axis depends on biological scale.

**Claim ceiling:** present ecological correspondence, not selection, plasticity, reaction norm or historical cause.

## Figure 4 — Calendar identifiability collapses the causal resolution

**Question:** What happens when the best-bounded orientation event is pushed from relative history into calendar time and palaeolocation?

**Primary panels**
- A: evidence chain showing only one current capitulum event reaches `transition -> chronology -> palaeolocation -> historical environment`.
- B: 94 chronology × four-region envelope for the orientation event.
- C: central 0.79–0.74 Ma climate direction versus full-envelope classification.
- D: paired regional ordering sensitivity: southern Japan rank 1 in 48/94, pairwise wins 61/94, 61/94 and 64/94, below the 75% gate.
- E: matched-window level/change/variability and global-sea-level failure summary.

**Headline:** an intuitively coherent central trajectory remains a tendency, not a robust historical trigger, after age and location uncertainty are propagated.

**Claim ceiling:** deterministic sensitivity coverage, not posterior event age or ancestral-area probability.

## Figure 5 — Evidence hierarchy: assembly is more identifiable than recurring cause

**Question:** Does one coarse historical regime recur across representative East-Asian differentiations?

**Primary panels**
- A: 17-BIOCLIM lineage atlas: 0/324 robust event-level classes.
- B: three-clade global sea-level diagnostic: 0/21 robust event-metric classes.
- C: final evidence staircase:
  `configuration diversity -> repeated change -> relative depth -> named localization -> calendar time -> historical cause`.

Use positive visual weight on the first three layers; the figure should not read as a wall of zeroes.

**Headline:** repeated mosaic phenotypic assembly is well resolved relative to the much weaker identification of one recurring coarse historical cause.

**Claim ceiling:** rejects/reduces the tested universal coarse-trigger class only; local geography, biotic interactions and lineage-specific mechanisms remain live.

# Figure-order rule

Do not open the paper with palaeoclimate or sea level. The visual story must establish **what was assembled** before showing **why its recurring cause becomes non-identifiable**.
