Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, “Repeated capitulum differentiation at unequal evolutionary depths without a recurring coarse historical trigger in a young thistle radiation,” for publication in the *Journal of Evolutionary Biology*.

The manuscript addresses a general problem in evolutionary inference: repeated phenotypic differentiation can be reconstructed more strongly than the historical process that caused it. Using a young East-Asian *Cirsium* radiation, we explicitly separate recurrence, relative evolutionary depth, calendar identifiability and historical environmental context rather than treating broad Pleistocene coincidence as evidence of a common selective trigger.

Three results motivate the manuscript. First, capitulum orientation, phyllary posture and involucre stickiness all differentiated repeatedly, but at unequal evolutionary depths: orientation requires four to six minimum changes, phyllary posture exactly three and stickiness exactly five, while zero of three trait pairs retains a robust shared transition-localization pattern. Second, only one capitulum transition currently passes the full public-data gate from a reconstructed trait change to bounded chronology, alternative palaeolocations and historical environment. For this core-*Nipponocirsium* orientation event, the central 0.79–0.74 Ma chronology suggests a coherent climate trajectory, but no tested climate direction, environmental level, absolute change, temporal variability or global eustatic sea-level metric survives the full chronology and palaeolocation uncertainty envelope. Third, broadening the test does not recover a universal coarse trigger: a 17-BIOCLIM diagnostic across six dated lineage contexts yields 0/324 robust event-level classes, and a three-clade global sea-level diagnostic yields 0/21.

The value of the study is therefore not a preferred environmental story but an explicit resolution boundary. Public evidence strongly resolves repeated differentiation and unequal historical depth, while one recurring tested coarse climatic or eustatic trigger remains unidentified. This negative result narrows the historical hypothesis space without implying that environment was irrelevant: local connectivity and fragmentation, lineage-specific exposure and biotic interactions remain incompletely measured.

The manuscript does not infer independent origins from minimum changes, adaptive convergence, exact trait-transition ages, ancestral-area probabilities, local land bridges, natural selection or reproductive-fitness benefit. It also does not require future field, RAD-seq or mechanism data to complete the present public-data paper. We believe this distinction between the identifiability of phenotypic history and historical cause is broadly relevant to studies of rapid radiation, repeated evolution and complex morphological structures.
