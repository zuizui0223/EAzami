Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Repeated mosaic assembly at unequal evolutionary depths in a young thistle radiation,”** for publication in the *Journal of Evolutionary Biology*.

The manuscript addresses a general problem in evolutionary biology: components of an integrated phenotype can share a present structure without sharing one synchronized evolutionary history. Using a young East-Asian *Cirsium* radiation, we reconstruct the historical assembly of three capitulum components and explicitly separate repeated differentiation, relative evolutionary depth, present ecological correspondence and historical-cause identifiability.

The main result is positive historical architecture. Thirty-six of 38 sampled Japanese concepts occur in one dominant radiation that retains multiple capitulum configurations. Orientation, phyllary posture and involucre stickiness each require repeated state change, but their minimum histories occupy different topology-only depths. On the same 1,000 bootstrap topologies, phyllary is deeper-permissive than stickiness in 1000/1000 realizations and deeper than orientation in 993/1000, while orientation is deeper than stickiness in 905/1000. Zero of three trait pairs passes our robust shared-transition-localization rule. We therefore identify repeated **mosaic historical assembly** rather than one synchronized whole-capitulum history.

We explicitly tested the strongest data-resolution alternative to this conclusion. When observed states for orientation and stickiness were deterministically reduced to the same n=10 coverage as phyllary while retaining the full 36-tip topology, the central phyllary-deeper ordering persisted in 97.5% of matched-orientation and 96.5% of matched-stickiness topology comparisons. The deepest matched tails still overlap, so the manuscript does not claim coverage independence or equal/unequal evolutionary rates.

Present ecological correspondence provides a complementary result rather than a historical-cause shortcut. Orientation–environment relationships are scale-partitioned: annual precipitation is supported among taxa but not within taxa, annual mean temperature is supported within taxa but not among taxa, and a directionally stable East-Asian precipitation-seasonality state contrast is not reproduced as a positive within-taxon response. The manuscript therefore does not treat one present-day coefficient as a universal historical driver.

Historical cause is less identifiable than phenotypic assembly. Only one current capitulum transition passes the full public-data chain from trait change to bounded chronology, palaeolocation scenarios and historical environment. Its central 0.79–0.74 Ma climate trajectory is coherent, but no tested climate direction, environmental level, absolute change, temporal variability or global sea-level metric survives the full uncertainty envelope. A broader 17-BIOCLIM lineage diagnostic yields 0/324 robust event-level classes, and a three-clade global sea-level diagnostic yields 0/21. These results constrain a single recurring **coarse tested** historical regime without implying that environment, local geography or biotic interactions were irrelevant.

The manuscript does not infer independent origins from minimum changes, adaptive convergence, developmental/genetic modularity, exact transition ages, ancestral-area probabilities, evolutionary rates, natural selection or reproductive-fitness benefit. We believe the explicit distinction between recoverable phenotypic assembly and less-identifiable historical cause is broadly relevant to rapid radiations and to the evolution of complex multicomponent phenotypes.
