Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, “Repeated mosaic assembly at unequal evolutionary depths in a young thistle radiation,” for publication in the *Journal of Evolutionary Biology*.

The manuscript addresses a general problem in evolutionary inference: components of a complex phenotype can function together in extant organisms without having been assembled through one synchronized evolutionary history, and a present ecological association need not identify the environmental regime under which a trait originated. Using a young East-Asian *Cirsium* radiation, we separate repeated differentiation, topology-only evolutionary depth, transition localization, present ecological organization, transition–niche concordance, and historical-regime persistence rather than treating ancestral-state reconstruction and trait–environment association as one inferential step.

Three results motivate the manuscript. First, capitulum orientation, phyllary posture and involucre stickiness each differentiated repeatedly but occupy unequal evolutionary depths. Paired comparisons on the same 1,000 bootstrap topologies place phyllary deeper than stickiness in 1000/1000 realizations and deeper than orientation in 993/1000, while orientation is deeper than stickiness in 905/1000. Equalizing observed-state coverage retains the central ordering but reveals overlap in the deepest tails, and zero of three trait pairs passes the robust shared-transition-localization rule. The resulting biological picture is repeated **mosaic assembly**, not one persistent whole-capitulum history.

Second, orientation shows a specific transition-level present-niche pattern. After freezing the composite U→D direction as higher precipitation seasonality plus lower annual mean temperature (`BIO15 up + BIO1 down`), inferred transition probabilities aligned with that regime on all six accepted topologies. In the 12-taxon primary panel, only 16/792 count-preserving state maps were at least as extreme as observed; the original strict nine-taxon panel retained the result at 4/126. The reverse D→U direction tracked the opposite side of the same regime, with a strict bidirectional floor rank of 3/126. The U→D result also survived linear latitude/longitude residualization and internal-edge-only scoring simultaneously (3/126), while a Japan-only panel was directionally consistent but not exceptional (10/56). The supported object is therefore a distributed East-Asian **composite transition–niche correspondence**, not a single-axis climatic effect or a universal Japan-only rule.

Third, the present regime does not simply project backward to origin. For the only orientation event that can currently be linked to a bounded chronology and palaeolocation envelope, the present `BIO15 up + BIO1 down` regime matched only 99/376 chronology × region scenarios. At the central 0.79–0.74 Ma chronology, BIO1 moved in the present-regime direction but BIO15 moved in the opposite direction in all four regional scenarios. Broader historical diagnostics likewise yield 0/324 robust climate event classes and 0/21 robust sea-level event-metric classes. The manuscript therefore identifies a contrast between **present transition–niche tracking** and **historical origin-regime non-persistence**, rather than turning a current association into a historical causal claim.

The study also contributes an inference framework. Reconstruction-aware nulls, exact minimum-history relative-depth envelopes, paired-topology comparisons, coverage-matched masking, history-conditioned counterfactuals, directional transition–environment decomposition, and a fixed historical-regime persistence test are used as distinct inferential layers. The methodological message is not a new ancestral-state reconstruction algorithm; it is that different evolutionary questions require different nulls and different uncertainty conditioning, and that present ecological tracking should be tested separately from historical origin.

The manuscript does not infer independent origins from minimum changes, adaptive convergence, equal or unequal evolutionary rates, exact transition ages, ancestral-area probabilities, local land bridges, natural selection, ancestry-independent climatic effects or reproductive-fitness benefit. Exact state-map fractions are finite exhaustive ranks under declared constraints, not biological-replicate P values or independent confirmations. Internal-edge environmental values remain reconstructions from present-day taxon niche centroids rather than observations of ancestral environments.
