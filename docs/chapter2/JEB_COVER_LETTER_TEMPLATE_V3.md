Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, “Repeated mosaic assembly at unequal evolutionary depths in a young thistle radiation,” for publication in the *Journal of Evolutionary Biology*.

The manuscript addresses a general problem in evolutionary inference: components of a complex phenotype can function together in extant organisms without having been assembled through one synchronized evolutionary history. Using a young East-Asian *Cirsium* radiation, we separate repeated differentiation, topology-only evolutionary depth, transition localization, present ecological correspondence and historical-cause identifiability rather than treating ancestral-state reconstruction as one inferential step.

Three results motivate the manuscript. First, capitulum orientation, phyllary posture and involucre stickiness each differentiated repeatedly but occupy unequal evolutionary depths. Paired comparisons on the same 1,000 bootstrap topologies place phyllary deeper than stickiness in 1000/1000 realizations and deeper than orientation in 993/1000, while orientation is deeper than stickiness in 905/1000. Equalizing observed-state coverage retains the central ordering but reveals overlap in the deepest tails, and zero of three trait pairs passes the robust shared-transition-localization rule. The resulting biological picture is repeated **mosaic assembly**, not one persistent whole-capitulum history.

Second, present orientation ecology is organized but both scale- and history-dependent. Annual precipitation is supported among taxa but not within taxa, whereas annual mean temperature is supported within taxa but not among taxa. A large East-Asian precipitation-seasonality contrast is directionally stable, yet a counterfactual conditioning analysis shows why that contrast cannot be read as an ancestry-independent climatic effect. Among all 126 state-count-preserving maps, only 5/126 are at least as positive as observed; after requiring the observed recurrence profile this becomes 3/40, and among the 10 maps with the nearest recurrence-plus-depth histories it becomes 3/10. Thus apparent ecological extremeness weakens as more of the observed evolutionary geometry is preserved.

This counterfactual conditioning ladder is also a methodological contribution. It compares a trait–environment association against progressively more realistic alternative trait maps:

`state frequency -> recurrence -> recurrence + relative evolutionary depth`.

The framework asks whether an ecological association remains exceptional after conditioning on the historical structure of the observed trait map. In the present case it prevents an overstrong climatic interpretation and identifies the ecological pattern as lineage/history embedded.

Third, historical cause is less identifiable than phenotypic assembly. Only one orientation transition currently passes the full public-data chain from reconstructed trait change to bounded chronology, alternative palaeolocations and historical environment. No tested climate or global sea-level metric survives the full admitted uncertainty envelope; broader diagnostics yield 0/324 robust climate event classes and 0/21 robust sea-level event-metric classes. These outcomes do not imply that environment was irrelevant. They define how far the present public evidence can support event-specific historical explanation.

We believe the study contributes both an empirical result and an inference framework. Empirically, a multidimensional reproductive structure was assembled repeatedly and mosaically at unequal evolutionary depths. Methodologically, topology sensitivity, coverage matching and history-conditioned counterfactuals are used as distinct tests of robustness rather than collapsed into one ancestral reconstruction or one environmental narrative.

The manuscript does not infer independent origins from minimum changes, adaptive convergence, equal or unequal evolutionary rates, exact transition ages, ancestral-area probabilities, local land bridges, natural selection, ancestry-independent climatic effects or reproductive-fitness benefit. The counterfactual fractions are finite conditional ranks rather than P values or independent confirmations.
