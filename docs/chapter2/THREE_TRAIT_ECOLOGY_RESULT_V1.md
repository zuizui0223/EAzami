# Three-trait present-ecology comparison v1

## Why this analysis exists

The historical paper compares orientation, phyllary posture and involucre stickiness, but earlier current-environment work was disproportionately orientation-centred. This analysis passes all three traits through the same present-environment collection and scoring pipeline.

Japan is used only as the population-provenance frame matching the Japan38 nuclear-tip analysis. It is not treated as an ecological region or a causal geographic category.

## Frozen design

- GBIF Japan occurrences for the authority-backed Japan38 taxon concepts;
- source-name guard, <=10 km coordinate uncertainty when available, 0.1-degree thinning;
- primary gate: >=3 thinned occurrences per taxon;
- CHELSA BIO1, BIO4, BIO12 and BIO15 only;
- taxon medians followed by complete state-label permutation ranks;
- no orientation-specific BIO15/BIO1 directional hypothesis is transferred to the other traits.

## Results

### Orientation

17 taxa (5 downward, 12 upward) entered the common panel. The omnibus four-climate separation ranked 1627/6188 = 26.29%. None of the four individual two-sided state contrasts was exceptional in this broader Japan-only tip-state analysis.

This is important because it shows that the previously recovered orientation ecology is not a generic static U-versus-D climate split. Its strongest evidence remains the separately frozen phylogeny-conditioned transition-level analysis.

### Phyllary posture

Only four taxa passed the primary gate: three ascending and one appressed. The finite label universe has only four maps, and the observed omnibus rank is 3/4. Reducing the occurrence requirement to one record adds two taxa but leaves appressed and spreading represented by only one lineage each; the omnibus rank is 30/30.

Therefore phyllary present ecology is genuinely not identifiable from the current public state-replicated panel. The bottleneck is not lack of environmental extraction but lack of replicated resolved posture states among occurrence-supported taxa.

### Stickiness

12 taxa entered the primary panel with balanced replication: six sticky and six nonsticky. The omnibus four-climate rank is 88/924 = 9.52%.

The strongest single axis is BIO12: sticky minus nonsticky = +1.311 standardized units, 22/924 = 2.38%. BIO15 is also positive (+1.048 SD; 70/924 = 7.58%), whereas BIO1 and BIO4 are weak.

This supports a focused ecological lead that sticky taxa occupy wetter current niches in the Japan provenance frame. Because four climate axes were frozen and inspected together, BIO12 should not be promoted as a standalone confirmatory discovery without the omnibus and multiplicity context.

## Cross-trait interpretation

The three traits do not fail in the same way and should not be represented as if orientation alone has ecology:

- **orientation:** strong history; weak simple static state-climate separation in the common panel; stronger transition-level ecological concordance;
- **stickiness:** strong history; replicated 6-vs-6 present-niche comparison; precipitation-related ecological lead;
- **phyllary posture:** strong history; present ecology remains resolution-limited because replicated state coverage is inadequate.

This changes the paper architecture. The ecological section should compare **forms of ecological evidence across traits**, not present orientation as the only interpretable trait. Orientation is the best transition-level case, stickiness supplies an independent present-niche case, and phyllary explicitly defines the current public-data ceiling.

## Claim ceiling

No result here establishes adaptation, selection or historical cause. Taxon-level states are not occurrence-level phenotypes. Phyllary coverage failure is not evidence of no ecological effect. The Japan frame is provenance matching, not a biological region definition.
