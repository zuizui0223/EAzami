Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, “A young Cirsium radiation repeatedly rebuilt its reproductive head from functionally distinct components,” for publication in the *Journal of Evolutionary Biology*.

The study asks how a complex reproductive structure is assembled during a young radiation. Orientation, phyllary posture and involucre stickiness each changed repeatedly, but their histories occupy different relative lineage depths and none of the three trait pairs shows robust shared transition localization. Independent experiments point to different candidate ecological interfaces for these components, while a *Cirsium* meta-analysis shows that reproductive insect antagonists can impose large seed-output costs. Orientation provides a focal ecological bridge: reconstructed transitions track a present BIO15-up/BIO1-down regime, but that regime fails as a model of the sole bounded historical origin event. We therefore separate recurrent functional rebuilding from the still-unresolved selective trigger of individual rebuilding events.

The manuscript is deliberately conservative about its causal ceiling. The orientation environmental vector is reported as a post-result focused hypothesis rather than an independent preregistered confirmation; the historical-origin analysis is limited to one calendarized event; trait histories retain unequal source-backed coverage and an undated phylogram; and functional experiments from other Asteraceae are used as mechanism evidence rather than transported effect sizes for East-Asian *Cirsium*. These limitations are explicit in the manuscript and Supporting Information rather than being used to inflate the adaptation claim.
