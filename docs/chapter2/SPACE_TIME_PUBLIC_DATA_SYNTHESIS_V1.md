# Chapter 2 space–time public-data synthesis v1

Status date: 2026-08-30

## Decision

The public-data programme is organized as one paired argument:

- **Azami = diversity breadth**: the present-day geographic and environmental breadth of coordinate-bearing continuous capitulum phenotypes;
- **EAzami = diversity depth**: the historical depth, recurrence and bounded ecological correspondence of the same decomposed phenotype inside the East-Asian radiation.

The paired logic is:

> **phenotype across space → phenotype through evolutionary history → explicit boundary of what public data can and cannot explain**

Chapter 2 is not required to turn every phenotype into an adaptive story. A trait can close as `priority_bridge`, `space_only`, `history_only`, `unresolved` or `not_evaluable`. This asymmetric result is more defensible than selecting only traits that tell the same positive story.

The machine-readable closure ledger is:

- `../../data/evidence/chapter2_space_time_public_data_closure_v1.csv`

## Common evidence ladder

The same four levels are applied to every trait.

1. **Spatial breadth** — does the continuous phenotype align with a present-day environmental gradient after the frozen Azami controls?
2. **Historical depth** — is a recurrent state history or continuous phylogenetic structure supported on the accepted East-Asian scaffold and uncertainty set?
3. **Cross-axis ecological correspondence** — does current state–ecology correspondence remain directionally stable after phylogenetic correction, topology propagation and species leave-one-out analysis?
4. **Adaptive mechanism** — does the trait change a specified function and reproductive fitness under the relevant selective environment?

Levels 1–3 can be addressed with current public data. Level 4 requires Chapter 3 measurements or experiments. Spatial association plus repeated history is not itself adaptation or convergence.

## Result 1 — orientation is the current space–time–ecology bridge

### Spatial breadth in Azami

At the among-taxon scale, larger signed head-axis angle relative to EXIF image vertical aligns with higher annual precipitation (`BIO12`):

- standardized coefficient: **+0.304359**;
- global-family `q`: **0.021**;
- broad-space coefficient: **+0.286086**;
- broad-space permutation `P`: **0.017**;
- same direction in all **52** audited historical-placement trees;
- same direction in every applicable sampling-composition perturbation.

This is an adaptive-pattern candidate under current controls, not a gravity-referenced measurement and not demonstrated rain protection.

### Historical depth in EAzami

Orientation has:

- **20** resolved concepts;
- ML minimum changes: **6**;
- UFBoot minimum-change range: **4–6**.

This establishes repeated state change as a lower-bound historical result. It does not establish independent origins or convergence.

### East-Asian ecological correspondence

In the frozen GBIF-only panel, downward/nodding taxa occupy niches with:

- higher precipitation seasonality (`BIO15`): downward minus upward = **+1.320 to +1.330 SD**;
- lower annual mean temperature (`BIO1`): downward minus upward = **−0.975 to −0.967 SD**;
- sign agreement across accepted topologies: **6/6** for both axes;
- species-LOO sign agreement: **54/54** for both axes.

The GBIF-only classification remains `unresolved` because the frozen thresholds are not crossed. In the direct-TBN-native sensitivity, the panel expands to 11 taxa and both axes become `tendency_supported`; in the broader non-GBIF-mirror TBN tier the class returns to `unresolved`. Across all admissible tiers, however, the BIO15 and BIO1 signs remain unchanged across every topology and species leave-one-out fit.

### Cross-axis synthesis

The strongest defensible synthesis is a **cross-scale hydric correspondence**:

- Azami detects orientation sorting along the **amount** of annual precipitation across the global sampled domain;
- EAzami detects orientation-state sorting along the **temporal unevenness** of precipitation inside the East-Asian radiation.

BIO12 and BIO15 are not interchangeable. Their agreement therefore should not be written as replication of one coefficient. It is better expressed as evidence that orientation is associated with more than one dimension of the rainfall regime. A plausible hypothesis is that broad climatic wetness and episodic wet-season exposure impose different components of reproductive wetting risk, but current public data do not measure flowering-period rainfall, interception, pollen performance or seed fitness.

The temperature results are not merged into the same narrative. Azami has a positive within-taxon orientation–BIO1 association, whereas EAzami places downward taxa in colder niches. This scale-dependent sign structure rules out a simple universal statement such as “downward heads are a cold-climate phenotype.”

## Result 2 — solar radiation sorts visible colour, but the observed direction is not a generic pigment confirmation

Azami identifies one robust visible-colour candidate:

- higher mean shortwave radiation (`rsds`) aligns with **lower CIELAB corolla chroma** among taxa;
- standardized coefficient: **−0.345372**;
- global-family `q`: **0.006**;
- broad-space coefficient: **−0.712411**;
- broad-space permutation `P`: **0.001**;
- residual Moran `P`: **0.217**;
- same linear direction in all **52** audited historical-placement trees.

The corresponding within-taxon RSDS–chroma estimate is approximately zero and unsupported. The current result is therefore among-taxon environmental sorting, not a demonstrated within-species radiation response.

This negative direction must not be translated into “more radiation produces more anthocyanin.” The endpoint is visible JPEG-derived CIELAB chroma, not spectral reflectance, UV pattern, anthocyanin concentration or pathway activity. External flower-colour studies also use different response constructs, including spectral contrast, UV-absorbing area and pigment chemistry. A difference in direction across those constructs is not a direct contradiction.

Current data leave at least four distinguishable possibilities:

1. a high-radiation strategy based on paler or more reflective visible petals;
2. pigment-composition or hue shifts that reduce visible chroma without reducing protective chemistry;
3. UV patterning that is invisible to the current RGB endpoint;
4. residual image, illumination or flower-background measurement structure.

The public-data result is therefore:

> **Solar radiation is associated with among-taxon sorting of visible corolla chroma, and the negative direction rejects a simple universal “higher radiation → higher visible saturation” model.**

It is not yet a historical colour-transition result. In the present Japan38 continuous-history analysis, no colour unit receives corrected two-sided phylogenetic-structure support. The high-depth lightness subset has a raw negative phylogenetic-distance association, but it does not survive the frozen family correction and does not replicate in the source-balanced Japanese sensitivity. Colour consequently closes as a strong spatial candidate and a time-axis boundary, not as demonstrated repeated adaptive evolution.

## Result 3 — the traits close asymmetrically

| Trait | Spatial breadth | Historical depth | Current ecology | Cross-axis class |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Orientation | robust BIO12 among-taxon candidate | recurrent; 4–6 minimum changes | BIO15/BIO1 signs robust; support class source-sensitive | **priority bridge** |
| Visible colour | robust negative chroma–RSDS candidate | continuous Japanese history unresolved | not joined to resolved colour events | **space only / time boundary** |
| Phyllary posture | image geometry is not homologous posture coding | exactly 3 minimum changes | `not_evaluable` | **history only / ecology boundary** |
| Stickiness | no calibrated continuous Azami endpoint | exactly 5 minimum changes | `not_evaluable` | **history only / ecology boundary** |
| Outline shape | coordinate-bearing continuous phenotype measured | no corrected continuous-history support | no validated performance axis | **space-and-time negative boundary** |
| Involucre architecture / armature | multiple spatial image endpoints; botanical calibration required | no single canonical homologous history | not evaluable | **measurement/history boundary** |

This table is not a ranking of biological importance. It records which part of the evidence chain current public assets can actually identify.

## What can be said about adaptive significance

The public-data synthesis supports a **multi-pathway adaptive hypothesis**, not a demonstrated adaptation claim.

Orientation is the strongest candidate because three independent layers now meet:

> recurrent history + global hydric spatial sorting + East-Asian precipitation-seasonality correspondence

Independent experimental literature makes wetting protection, pollen viability, thermal timing and pollinator presentation plausible pathways. The present analysis does not choose among them. The correct wording is:

> **Repeated orientation states are environmentally non-random with respect to multiple dimensions of hydric regime, making reproductive exposure and presentation high-priority mechanisms for direct testing.**

Colour likewise remains potentially multifunctional — pollinator signalling, visible/UV optical properties and pigment physiology can all matter — but current data connect only visible chroma to radiation sorting. Its evolutionary recurrence and causal pathway remain unresolved.

Phyllary posture and stickiness show the reverse evidence pattern: recurrence is strong, while ecology is not evaluable. They demonstrate that repeated history alone cannot identify the selective axis.

The general conclusion is therefore not “every capitulum trait is adaptive.” It is:

> **Different capitulum components occupy different positions along the space–history–ecology evidence chain, consistent with a partly modular and selection-mosaic view of capitulum diversification.**

“Consistent with” is essential. Current public data do not establish modular genetic architecture, fitness surfaces or the causal selective agents.

## Public-data closure rule

The existing public-data scientific core is complete when all of the following remain visible in the submission package:

1. the full Azami spatial atlas, including unsupported and unexecuted rows;
2. the EAzami minimum-change and relative-depth histories;
3. ecological reach classified as `tendency_supported`, `unresolved` or `not_evaluable` without converting missing overlap into a null relationship;
4. the Taiwan occurrence-source sensitivity without outcome-selected promotion of the direct-TBN tier;
5. the trait-level closure ledger above, preserving the asymmetric evidence chain.

Remaining work inside the public-data chapter is therefore package integration and validation, not an unrestricted search for additional positive associations. Any future replacement of the GBIF-only orientation primary must first freeze an outcome-independent multi-source occurrence harmonization contract. Mechanism, fitness, calibrated colour, botanical homology and population-matched transition tests belong to Chapter 3.

## Canonical programme conclusion

> **Azami resolves the breadth of present-day capitulum diversity across space; EAzami resolves its depth and repeated assembly through evolutionary history. Together they show that capitulum components are selectively organized across space and asymmetrically resolved through time. Orientation currently provides the strongest bridge between both axes through a robust hydric direction, while colour, phyllary posture, stickiness and shape expose explicit boundaries that define the next causal tests.**
