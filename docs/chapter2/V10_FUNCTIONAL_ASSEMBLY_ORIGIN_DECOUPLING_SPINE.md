# Chapter 2 V10 — recurrent functional assembly and origin–maintenance decoupling

Status: ACTIVE REFRAME  
Date: 2026-10-03

## Core biological question

> **How is a complex capitulum repeatedly assembled during a young radiation, are its repeatedly changing components plausible functional traits, and can the environmental trigger of their origin be identified?**

This replaces the V9.6 scale-conditioned ecology framing. The separate Azami image-angle dataset is excluded from this paper.

## Main answer

> **The capitulum was repeatedly assembled from components with unequal evolutionary histories. Independent functional evidence makes those components plausible ecological interfaces rather than arbitrary morphological markers, but recurrence itself is not treated as evidence of adaptation. Orientation provides the strongest focal ecological test: reconstructed changes track a present BIO15-up/BIO1-down niche regime, yet that regime is not recovered at the sole calendarized origin event. Thus present ecological association can characterize an evolved trait without identifying the environmental trigger that generated it.**

## Logic of inference

### Q1. Was capitulum diversity repeatedly assembled within the radiation?

Yes.

- 36/38 Japanese taxon concepts occur in the dominant radiation.
- orientation: 4–6 minimum state changes;
- phyllary posture: exactly 3;
- stickiness: exactly 5.

**Claim:** repeated differentiation occurred within the radiation.

**Not claimed:** repeated change by itself is non-random, adaptive or convergent.

### Q2. Was the whole capitulum repeatedly transformed as one unit?

No simple synchronized history is supported.

- phyllary deeper-permissive than stickiness: 1000/1000 paired topologies;
- phyllary deeper-permissive than orientation: 993/1000;
- orientation deeper-permissive than stickiness: 905/1000;
- complete central ordering: 898/1000;
- robust shared transition localization: 0/3 trait pairs.

**Claim:** capitulum assembly is historically mosaic.

### Q3. Are the repeatedly changing components biologically plausible functional traits?

The evidence supports distinct **functional-interface hypotheses**, with unequal strength.

#### Orientation — abiotic reproductive exposure / timing

Focal evidence:
- East-Asian U→D transitions track BIO15-up + BIO1-down;
- strict exact rank 4/126;
- bidirectional floor rank 3/126;
- combined geography-residual + internal-edge stress 3/126.

Closest direct manipulation:
- *Cremanthodium campanulatum*: experimentally erect capitula produce fewer achenes;
- water and UV-B reduce pollen viability;
- no pollinator preference between erect and nodding treatments;
- no meaningful orientation-driven internal thermal difference.

**Leading domain:** abiotic reproductive exposure plus timing.

**Boundary:** external Asteraceae manipulation supports mechanism plausibility, not adaptation of East-Asian *Cirsium* orientation.

#### Phyllary posture — mechanical antagonist access

Closest direct manipulations:
- *Centaurea solstitialis*: removing involucral spines removes deterrence of illegitimate Lepidoptera and reduces filled seeds by 22%; legitimate visitation frequency does not increase;
- *Pedicularis rex*: draining protective bracts increases seed predation in 5/6 populations and lowers final seed set without detectable pollinator discrimination.

**Leading domain:** mechanical antagonist exclusion.

**Boundary:** direct focal *Cirsium* phyllary manipulation is absent.

#### Stickiness — arthropod-community filtering under trait cost

Direct *Cirsium* evidence is context dependent:
- sticky-exudate occlusion increases seed predation in one *C. discolor* experiment;
- no effect in *C. flodmani* and later *C. discolor* tests;
- pollinators avoid trap contact, several seed predators bypass it, ants/aphids are deterred and predatory arthropods can use sticky inflorescences.

**Leading domain:** local arthropod-community filtering under benefit/cost trade-off.

**Boundary:** universal sticky-defence adaptation is contradicted as a general rule.

### Q4. Is there enough fitness leverage for these interfaces to matter?

Across four independent *Cirsium* reproductive-herbivory experimental programmes:

- pooled seed-output RR under reduced versus ambient insect herbivory = 2.674;
- 95% CI = 2.388–2.993;
- equivalent ambient-herbivory loss of potential seed output = 62.6%;
- I² = 1.02%.

**Claim:** reproductive antagonists can impose large fitness costs in *Cirsium*.

**Boundary:** this meta-analysis does not assign that fitness cost to orientation, phyllary posture or stickiness.

### Q5. Does orientation show a non-random ecological correspondence at evolutionary transitions?

Yes, under the declared conditional finite-map estimator.

Fixed post-result U→D vector:
- BIO15 up;
- BIO1 down.

Exact ranks:
- n≥5: 16/792 = 2.02%;
- n≥3: 19/1716 = 1.11%;
- n≥10: 4/126 = 3.17%.

The reverse transition tracks the opposite side of the same vector:
- bidirectional-floor rank: 3/126 = 2.38%.

Directional sign survives all single-taxon deletions, geography residualization and internal-edge-only scoring.

**Claim:** orientation transition placement is ecologically structured under the current estimator.

**Boundary:** finite-map exceptionality is not proof of selection or adaptation.

### Q6. Was the same climate regime the historical trigger of orientation origin?

No support for persistence of that present regime.

For the sole U→D event with bounded chronology + palaeolocation:

- 94 chronology pairs × 4 regions = 376 scenarios;
- present-regime sign match = 99/376 = 26.3%;
- Taiwan = 21.3%;
- Ryukyu corridor = 9.6%;
- southern Japan = 43.6%;
- East-Asian core corridor = 30.9%;
- only 6/94 chronologies match in all four regions.

At the central 0.79–0.74 Ma chronology:
- BIO1 decreases in 4/4 regions;
- BIO15 also decreases in 4/4 regions;
- therefore the present BIO15-up/BIO1-down U→D regime fails in 4/4 central scenarios.

Broader historical diagnostics:
- 17 BIOCLIM variables;
- six dated lineage contexts;
- 15,472 scenario × variable combinations;
- robust event-level climate classes: 0/324;
- global sea-level event-metric classes: 0/21.

**Claim:** no recurring coarse tested climatic or global eustatic trigger is identified.

**Stronger conceptual result:** the environmental regime associated with an evolved state today need not identify the environment in which that state originated.

## Adaptation ladder

The paper must keep these levels separate:

1. **recurrent evolution** — established;
2. **mosaic assembly** — established;
3. **functional plausibility** — supported by direct external/within-genus experiments, strength differs by trait;
4. **focal ecological transition correspondence** — supported for orientation;
5. **historical selective trigger** — not identified;
6. **adaptation in focal East-Asian *Cirsium*** — not demonstrated.

The paper therefore does not use “repeated = adaptive.”

## Discussion spine

1. **Complex reproductive heads are repeatedly assembled, not simply inherited as fixed syndromes.**
2. **Repeated components are plausible functional interfaces, but recurrence is not itself evidence of adaptation.**
3. **Orientation supplies the focal bridge from evolutionary history to ecology.**
4. **Current ecological association and historical origin are different estimands.**
5. **Failure to recover a common historical trigger suggests changing selective regimes, exaptation, ecological sorting or unresolved local/biotic causes—not environmental irrelevance.**
6. **Direct focal manipulations are required to cross from functional plausibility to adaptation.**

## One-sentence paper claim

> **A young thistle radiation repeatedly assembled a complex reproductive head from components with different evolutionary histories and plausible ecological functions; orientation transitions track a present environmental regime, but that regime fails as a model of the trait's historical origin, separating functional ecological association from the trigger of evolutionary innovation.**
