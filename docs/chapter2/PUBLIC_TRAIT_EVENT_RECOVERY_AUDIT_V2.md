# Chapter 2 public trait-event recovery audit v2

Status: 2026-08-31

## Purpose

This audit asks how far public dated phylogenies, taxonomic morphology, distribution histories and Azami spatial results can jointly recover the evolutionary depth and possible causes of capitulum diversity before Chapter 3 introduces own ancestry-linked population data.

The central distinction is:

> **A dated lineage split is not automatically a dated trait transition, and a dated range event is not automatically the selective pressure.**

Every public record is therefore classified as one of:

1. a dated or branch-bounded trait-transition candidate;
2. a conditional transition whose direction depends on topology or ancestral state;
3. a dated sister-lineage phenotype contrast without reconstructed trait history;
4. a lineage-polymorphic/non-identifiable trait history;
5. a biogeographic or demographic exposure opportunity without a mapped trait transition.

The machine-readable audit is `data/evidence/chapter2_public_trait_event_recovery_audit_v2.csv`.

## 1. What public chronology adds

### Orientation

Japan38 already requires 4–6 orientation changes across the bootstrap tree ensemble, but that tree is measured in substitutions/site. Public East-Asian studies add calendar-time constraints to one major Nipponocirsium state contrast.

A restricted six-taxon scaffold places one erect-to-nodding change on the Taiwan-trio stem. Adding public Japanese core Nipponocirsium changes the parsimonious placement: erect *C. morii* is basal, whereas both Japanese core and Taiwan core Nipponocirsium are nodding. The likely change therefore lies on the core-Nipponocirsium stem after the *C. morii* split and before the Japanese–Taiwan core split.

The two central ages, approximately 0.79 and 0.74 Ma, come from separate analyses. Their marginal intervals overlap, so they cannot be treated as a joint 0.74–0.79 Ma posterior event interval. The pre-split geographic setting is unresolved. Thus public data narrow chronology but do not yet identify the environment of orientation origin.

### Flower colour

The public six-taxon East-Asian scaffold yields two branch-bounded, conditional coloured-to-white histories:

- *C. brevicaule*: a terminal branch envelope from the present to the approximately 0.93 Ma *C. brevicaule*–*C. irumtiense* split;
- *C. kawakamii*: a terminal branch envelope from the present to the approximately 0.35 Ma *C. kawakamii*–*C. tatakaense* split on the published primary topology, or to 0.47 Ma under the union of frozen within-trio resolutions.

These are not exact event ages. The *C. brevicaule* direction remains conditional on ancestral colour, and the *C. kawakamii* direction remains conditional on topology and fixed tip coding.

Within the Taiwanese *C. japonicum* complex, white and bluish-purple populations occur within the same lineage and reticulation is documented. This record is not usable as a species-tip colour transition. It shows that public species labels can erase the population variation needed to reconstruct colour history.

## 2. A new public-data hypothesis: white lineages and broader head remodelling

Two phylogenetically separated white–coloured sister systems provide a directionally repeated extant contrast.

### Arenicola

White *C. brevicaule* versus bluish-purple *C. irumtiense*:

- split central estimate approximately 0.93 Ma;
- inner:outer phyllary length ratio 2.24 versus 1.44;
- clearly tiered versus weakly tiered involucre;
- floret length 2.94 versus 2.59 cm.

### Taiwan Nipponocirsium

White *C. kawakamii* versus purple *C. tatakaense*:

- split central estimate approximately 0.35 Ma;
- phyllary length 1.80 versus 1.04 cm;
- the white lineage is reported to have longer florets, corolla lobes and synanthers;
- phyllary count 99 versus 154 and floret count 138 versus 254.

Across the two systems, the white lineage has longer florets and stronger or longer phyllary differentiation. However, the phyllary variables are not homologous measurements across studies: one is an inner:outer ratio/tiering measure and the other is absolute phyllary length. Head counts also differ in direction from simple “larger display” expectations. These are two dated sister contrasts, not reconstructed simultaneous transitions.

The safe public-data hypothesis is therefore:

> **Repeated white lineages may be embedded in broader, system-specific remodelling of floral and involucre architecture rather than representing pigment state alone.**

This is a Chapter 3 candidate, not a Chapter 2 syndrome result. Public data do not yet establish correlated evolution, a shared developmental mechanism, a common selective pressure, transition order, adaptation or fitness consequences.

The corresponding table is `data/evidence/chapter2_white_lineage_sister_contrast_v1.csv`.

## 3. How this connects to Azami

Azami identifies a strong present spatial association between visible corolla chroma and surface shortwave radiation. The dated white-lineage contrasts add evolutionary depth, but no directly commensurate historical surface-RSDS series currently exists. Orbital insolation or a radiative model would be a separate model layer and cannot be called a direct replication of Azami RSDS.

Therefore the current colour evidence chain is:

`strong present RSDS–chroma sorting`

→ `two conditional dated local white-lineage histories`

→ `two white–coloured sister contrasts with broader floral/involucre differences`

→ `historical radiative driver not identifiable from current public data`.

This is stronger than a space-only colour result because it identifies tractable evolutionary systems, but weaker than an adaptive explanation because chronology, population ancestry, historical radiation exposure and fitness are not joined in one estimand.

For orientation, Azami BIO12 and EAzami present BIO15/BIO1 remain the strongest present ecological bridge. The public chronology audit shows that adding taxa changes the inferred geographic setting of the historical transition, so current descendant niches cannot be substituted for origin climate. That negative result is itself part of the Chapter 2 contribution.

## 4. Distribution process versus selective pressure

Public biogeographic dates are retained as exposure opportunities:

- dominant Middle-Asia-to-Japan founder event and rapid radiation;
- Japanese–Taiwan Nipponocirsium divergence;
- separate *C. dipsacolepis* arrival;
- *C. lineare* East-Asia-to-Japan expansion;
- demographic/range shifts within Taiwanese complexes.

They are not assigned as trait-transition dates unless the transition-bearing branch is independently reconciled. The process model remains:

`range reorganisation → changed abiotic/biotic exposure opportunity → possible trait transition`.

Range reorganisation is neither the selective agent nor proof of adaptation.

## 6. Public-data stop rule and Chapter 3 handoff

Public-data expansion should stop when a record cannot join all of the following without inventing equivalence:

1. population- or voucher-resolved phenotype;
2. ancestry/topology;
3. branch-bounded timing;
4. geographic exposure scenario;
5. commensurate environmental history;
6. mechanism or fitness.

The most valuable Chapter 3 design generated by this audit is not a larger generic climate screen. It is ancestry-linked replication across the two white-lineage systems and orientation contrasts, measuring:

- calibrated visible and UV colour;
- pigment chemistry and floral expression;
- floret length and display architecture;
- homologous phyllary tiering/length/count;
- actual flowering-season radiation, rain/wetting and temperature;
- pollinator effective contact and antagonist exposure;
- filled-achene fitness.

This design can test whether colour and architecture are coupled by a common developmental route, independently co-selected, or merely co-occurring in the two public sister contrasts.

## Claim ceiling

This audit does not establish a white-flower syndrome, correlated evolution, modular adaptation, convergent adaptation, transition order, historical radiation causation or reproductive fitness. It converts public-data fragments into explicit event classes and falsifiable Chapter 3 comparisons while preserving non-identifiability.
