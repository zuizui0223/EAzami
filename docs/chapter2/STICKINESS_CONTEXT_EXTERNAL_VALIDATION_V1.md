# Stickiness context external validation v1

Status date: 2026-09-05

This is a **post-freeze validation** of the already-selected model `S3_enemy_dependent_defence_cost_balance` from `chapter2_trait_cause_falsification_v1.json`. The S1/S2/S3 model set and decision rules were fixed before this literature validation was recovered.

## Prediction carried forward from the frozen model

If involucral/inflorescence stickiness is a context-dependent defence–cost trait rather than a universally beneficial defence or a universally costly trait, direct *Cirsium* manipulations should be able to produce defensive benefit in some species/year contexts and null effects in others.

## Direct Cirsium validation

### Willson, Anderson & Thomas (1983)

*Bracteal Exudates in Two Cirsium Species as Possible Deterrents to Insect Consumers of Seeds*. *American Midland Naturalist* 110(1): 212–214.

- In *Cirsium discolor* in central Illinois in 1980, experimentally occluding sticky bracteal exudates significantly increased insect seed predation: a **defensive-benefit** context.
- In *Cirsium flodmani* in northwestern Minnesota, the same general manipulation produced **no detected effect** in 1980 or 1982.

### Thomas (2003)

*Sticky Exudates on the Inflorescences of Cirsium discolor (Asteraceae) and Penstemon digitalis (Scrophulariaceae) as Possible Defense Against Seed Predators*. *The Great Lakes Entomologist* 36: 112–121. DOI: 10.22543/0090-0222.2085.

Across the 1982–1987 study series, neutralizing sticky traps in *C. discolor* did not increase seed predators, did not reduce predatory arthropods, and did not reduce seed production: a **null** context.

## Result

The direct genus-level validation contains both **benefit** and **null** outcomes, including different temporal evidence for *C. discolor*. This is the qualitative pattern predicted by the frozen context-dependent model and is incompatible with treating stickiness as a universally positive defence effect.

It does **not** prove that local enemy pressure caused the shallow Japan38 stickiness transitions. The North American experiments are not transported as East-Asian effect sizes, and the relevant antagonist community, secretion cost and pollinator cost remain unmeasured for JPN15/JPN06.

## Focal next falsifier

The highest-information causal test remains **within-JPN15 neutralization of natural stickiness versus sham**, jointly measuring:

- antagonist access / oviposition / damage;
- legitimate pollinator effective transfer;
- treatment and secretion-production costs;
- mature and viable seed output.

The 100/100 JPN06–JPN15 sister contrast and JPN06 terminal-history concentration provide ancestry-aware design leverage, not causality by themselves.
