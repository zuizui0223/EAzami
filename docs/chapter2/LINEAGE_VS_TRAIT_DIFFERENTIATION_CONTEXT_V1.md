# Lineage diversification context versus capitulum-trait trigger

Status date: 2026-09-01

## Decision

Chapter 2 separates two biological levels that must not be collapsed.

### A. Lineage diversification context

Published phylogenomic/biogeographic studies provide comparatively strong evidence that the young East-Asian/Japanese *Cirsium* radiation was assembled during Pleistocene dispersal, geographic isolation, glacial oscillation and island fragmentation.

- Moreyra et al. (2025; DOI 10.1016/j.ympev.2025.108285) place the dominant Japanese radiation after a jump dispersal to Japan at about 2.4 Ma (95% CI 1.7–3.6 Ma) and discuss geographic isolation, ecological specialization and changing island connectivity during Pleistocene sea-level fluctuations as diversification processes.
- Chang et al. (2026; DOI 10.1186/s12870-026-08097-6) date major East-Asian subsection divergences to roughly 1.30–1.02 Ma, *C. morii* versus core Nipponocirsium to ~0.79 Ma, Arenicola *C. brevicaule* versus *C. irumtiense* to ~0.93 Ma, and Japanese versus Taiwanese Sinocirsium to ~0.44 Ma. Their synthesis explicitly emphasizes Quaternary glaciations, island fragmentation and lineage-specific genomic change. The Miyako Strait is a strong phylogeographic break between the Arenicola sisters.
- Chang et al. (2025; DOI 10.1186/s40529-025-00454-2) date Japanese versus Taiwanese Nipponocirsium to ~0.74 Ma, *C. pengii* divergence to ~0.47 Ma and *C. kawakamii* versus *C. tatakaense* to ~0.35 Ma, with glacial/refugial isolation discussed as diversification context.

These results support **lineage-level exposure and isolation context**. They do not by themselves identify why a particular capitulum trait changed.

### B. Trait-specific differentiation trigger

A capitulum-trait trigger requires a stricter chain:

`trait transition -> bounded branch/time -> plausible paleolocation -> historical environment/process -> matched background -> recurrence across independent homologous events`

Current public evidence passes the full calendar+paleolocation+historical-environment gate for only one trait transition: the core-Nipponocirsium erect/upward -> nodding/downward orientation event.

For that event, the single central 0.79 -> 0.74 Ma pair gives a visually coherent climate pattern, but the full 94 chronology x 4 paleolocation envelope removes every tested robust signed BIO1/BIO4/BIO12/BIO15 direction. Therefore the lineage-level Pleistocene context cannot be promoted to a trait-specific climate trigger.

## Why the Mid-Pleistocene Transition is context, not a test result

The orientation chronology is centered near ~0.8 Ma, but its uncertainty envelope itself makes Mid-Pleistocene overlap almost inevitable:

- 56/94 admissible chronology pairs span exactly 0.800 Ma;
- 90/94 overlap a broad 0.700–0.900 Ma band;
- 78/94 overlap 0.750–0.850 Ma;
- 71/94 overlap 0.770–0.830 Ma.

Thus `the event occurred around the Mid-Pleistocene Transition` is a useful temporal description but is not independent evidence that the MPT triggered orientation differentiation.

## Sea level and geographic isolation

A frozen NOAA Spratt–Lisiecki global sea-level sensitivity exists for the former restricted 0.47–0.79 Ma Taiwan descendant window. That interval was not unusually volatile versus same-duration windows (SD percentile 0.0135; range percentile 0.0906). This is a useful negative boundary, but it is not a local Ryukyu/Taiwan connectivity reconstruction and it is not the refined 94-scenario origin envelope.

Therefore:

- global sea-level fluctuation can be a **range-reorganization opportunity**;
- published island barriers can be **lineage-isolation context**;
- neither is automatically a **trait selective pressure**;
- a local land-bridge/fragmentation trigger requires bathymetry/geology plus an event-specific lineage location and timing model.

## Evidence hierarchy for V6

1. **Radiation context:** dispersal, Pleistocene climatic oscillation and island geography.
2. **Trait recurrence:** minimum-change lower bounds.
3. **Trait temporal depth:** internal/terminal and relative lineage-depth envelope.
4. **Event identifiability:** calendar age and paleolocation where defensible.
5. **Historical alignment:** level, signed change, absolute change and variability versus matched windows.
6. **Repeated trigger:** at least two independently bounded homologous transitions with compatible robust historical direction/process.
7. **Adaptive explanation:** direct trait-function and reproductive-fitness evidence in Chapter 3.

The key rule is that a stronger result at level 1 cannot be borrowed to fill a missing level 4–6 result.

## Current implication

The public literature already supports a meaningful Chapter 2 background claim:

> The focal thistle lineages diversified in a young Pleistocene setting shaped by dispersal, geographic isolation and recurrent island/climate reorganization.

The stronger capitulum-specific claim remains narrower:

> Capitulum modules were repeatedly differentiated at unequal evolutionary depths, but the historical trigger of those trait changes is only directly testable for a small subset of events and is currently unresolved for the bounded orientation event under full chronology and paleolocation uncertainty.

Neither statement establishes natural selection or adaptation.
