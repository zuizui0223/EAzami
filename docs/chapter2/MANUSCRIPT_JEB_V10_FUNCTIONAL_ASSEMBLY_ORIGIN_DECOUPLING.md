# Recurrent functional assembly of a complex reproductive head does not reveal its historical environmental trigger in a young thistle radiation

**Target journal:** *Journal of Evolutionary Biology* — Research Article  
**Status:** V10 functional-assembly / origin-decoupling manuscript draft  
**Running title:** Functional capitulum reassembly

## Abstract

Repeated evolution of a phenotype does not by itself establish adaptation, but recurrence becomes more informative when the changing components have plausible functions and their transitions show ecological structure. We tested this logic in the capitulum of a young *Cirsium* radiation. Orientation required four to six minimum state changes across 1,000 bootstrap topologies, phyllary posture exactly three and involucre stickiness exactly five. Their histories occupied unequal relative lineage depths, and zero of three trait pairs showed robust shared transition localization, supporting mosaic reassembly rather than synchronized whole-head evolution. Independent experiments identify distinct candidate interfaces: orientation with abiotic reproductive exposure, phyllary architecture with antagonist access and stickiness with context-dependent arthropod filtering. Across four independent *Cirsium* experiments, reducing reproductive insect herbivory increased seed output 2.674-fold. Orientation supplied a focal ecological bridge: reconstructed upward-to-downward transitions aligned with higher precipitation seasonality and lower temperature, with an exact strict-panel rank of 4/126. Yet this same environmental regime matched only 99/376 chronology-by-palaeolocation scenarios for the sole calendarized origin event and failed in all four regions at its central chronology. Broader historical screens recovered 0/324 robust climate classes and 0/21 robust sea-level classes. Thus functional ecological association in an evolved trait need not identify the environmental trigger of its origin.

**Keywords:** *Cirsium*; adaptation; capitulum; ecological function; evolutionary innovation; mosaic evolution; palaeoclimate; repeated evolution

# Introduction

Complex reproductive structures are assembled from components that can affect different interfaces with the environment. When the same component changes repeatedly across a radiation, an immediate temptation is to interpret recurrence as evidence of repeated adaptation. Recurrence alone, however, is insufficient: multiple state changes can arise without repeated selection, and minimum-change reconstructions do not count independent adaptive origins. A stronger inference requires separating whether a component changed repeatedly, whether it has a plausible effect on fitness, and whether its evolutionary transitions are associated with a reproducible ecological regime.

This distinction matters because the environment associated with an evolved state today need not be the environment that originally generated it. Selection can change through time, traits can be co-opted, ecological sorting can occur after origin, and present distributions can retain lineage and geographic structure. Present trait–environment correspondence and historical selective trigger are therefore different estimands. Testing whether they coincide requires linking reconstructed trait change to both present ecological structure and independently bounded historical environments.

The capitulum of *Cirsium* provides a useful system for this problem. Recent nuclear phylogenomics recovered a dominant Japanese radiation with extensive gene-tree discordance, while capitula vary in orientation, phyllary posture and involucre stickiness (Moreyra et al., 2025). These are not arbitrary measurements. Capitulum orientation can alter exposure of reproductive tissues to rain, wetting and radiation; involucral architecture can alter mechanical access to florets and developing seeds; and sticky exudates can alter arthropod access and community composition. At the same time, these components need not share one evolutionary history or one selective agent.

Here we ask five linked questions. First, were orientation, phyllary posture and stickiness repeatedly modified within the young radiation? Second, were those changes synchronized in evolutionary depth and branch localization, or was the capitulum assembled mosaically? Third, do independent manipulative studies support plausible functional pathways for the repeatedly changing components? Fourth, does the best-resolved component, orientation, show ecological structure at reconstructed evolutionary transitions? Fifth, does the same ecological regime recur at the historical origin event strongly enough to qualify as a candidate environmental trigger?

Our inferential ladder is deliberately asymmetric. Repeated change establishes recurrence, not adaptation. Functional experiments outside the focal transitions establish mechanism plausibility, not historical selection. Transition–environment concordance establishes ecological structure, not causation. Only persistence of a candidate regime at a bounded origin event would support that regime as a historical trigger. Failure at that final step is therefore biologically informative rather than a failed search.

# Materials and Methods

## Nuclear scaffold and capitulum states

The historical panel followed 38 Japanese taxon concepts represented by the nuclear phylogenomic framework of Moreyra et al. (2025). We used an independently reconstructed Compositae1061-compatible phylogram based on a frozen 241-locus starting set, yielding 236 quality-controlled loci, 176 rootable loci and a concatenated alignment of 161,654 bp. Maximum-likelihood inference used IQ-TREE 2.4.0 (Minh et al., 2020) with ModelFinder (Kalyaanamoorthy et al., 2017), 1,000 ultrafast-bootstrap replicates (Hoang et al., 2018) and 1,000 SH-aLRT replicates. Branch lengths were substitutions per site and were not interpreted as calendar time.

Thirty-six of the 38 sampled concepts occur within the dominant radiation. We analysed three source-backed discrete capitulum traits: orientation, phyllary posture and involucre stickiness. Missing and ambiguous states remained unresolved. Historical coverage comprised 20 concepts for orientation, 10 for phyllary posture and 13 for stickiness.

## Recurrence, evolutionary depth and shared transition localization

For the maximum-likelihood topology and each of 1,000 bootstrap topologies, we calculated unordered parsimony minima. These values are lower bounds on required state change and were not interpreted as counts of independent adaptive origins.

For every globally minimum-cost Sankoff history, relative lineage depth was defined for an edge subtending *d* descendants in a tree with *N* admitted tips as D = (N - d)/(N - 1). Terminal edges have *D* = 1, whereas smaller values permit deeper placement. Trait depth was compared directly on the same 1,000 bootstrap topologies. Because trait coverage differed, deterministic masking equalized observed-state coverage before repeating paired depth comparisons. Bootstrap trees are topology-sensitivity realizations, not independent biological replicates.

Shared-transition localization was evaluated with a branch-length-aware equal-rates Mk diagnostic and a topology-only sensitivity. A trait pair passed the robust descriptive rule only when positive localization overlap persisted under both treatments. The Mk layer was used as a diagnostic of transition localization rather than as proof of independent origins or adaptation (Lewis, 2001).

## Functional-interface evidence and reproductive fitness pressure

Functional evidence was ranked by organ homology, manipulation geometry and taxonomic proximity. For orientation, the closest direct angle manipulation is in *Cremanthodium campanulatum* (Niu & Sun, 2013), with *Cirsium purpuratum* studies providing within-genus pollination context (Ohashi & Yahara, 1998; Makino et al., 2007). For phyllary architecture, we used direct structural manipulations including the close-Cardueae *Centaurea solstitialis* experiment (Agrawal et al., 2000). For stickiness, we prioritized direct *Cirsium* exudate occlusion, neutralization and arthropod-guild studies (Willson et al., 1983; Thomas, 2003, 2007).

We treated these studies as mechanism evidence rather than transported effect sizes for East-Asian *Cirsium*. The frozen trait-specific synthesis assigned three leading functional domains: abiotic reproductive exposure/timing for orientation, mechanical antagonist access for phyllary posture and arthropod-community filtering under benefit/cost trade-offs for stickiness.

To quantify whether reproductive antagonism can impose a fitness pressure large enough to make access-related capitulum traits consequential, a frozen random-effects synthesis combined mature or viable seed output from four independent *Cirsium* experimental programmes that reduced reproductive insect herbivory (Louda & Potvin, 1995; Maron et al., 2002; West & Louda, 2018; Russell et al., 2025). Within-study strata and years were collapsed before across-study pooling. The estimand was seed output under experimentally reduced insect herbivory relative to ambient herbivory.

## Present ecological structure and orientation transition analysis

All three historical traits were first passed through one trait-blind present-environment workflow using BIO1, BIO4, BIO12, BIO15, short-wave radiation, vapour-pressure deficit, surface wind, growing-season precipitation and net primary productivity. Public GBIF occurrences were source-name checked, coordinate-quality controlled and thinned at 0.1 degrees. This common screen tested whether all three components collapse onto one present abiotic syndrome.

Orientation additionally had sufficient state-diverse public coverage for a phylogeny-conditioned transition analysis. After an earlier state comparison identified stable positive BIO15 and negative BIO1 directions, the upward-to-downward composite BIO15 up + BIO1 down was fixed as a post-result focused hypothesis. For each accepted topology, a symmetric two-state CTMC supplied edge transition probabilities and Brownian reconstruction supplied branch environmental differences. A transition-weighted composite statistic was ranked among every count-preserving orientation state map. Coverage sensitivities, single-taxon deletion, latitude/longitude residualization and internal-edge-only scoring were retained. The finite-map fractions are exhaustive conditional ranks, not *P* values from independent biological replicates.

A Japan/Taiwan source-partition diagnostic tested coarse data-source geography only; the source partitions were not interpreted as biological regions.

## Historical-origin test

Only one reconstructed upward-to-downward event passed the full public-data gate linking trait change to bounded chronology and palaeolocation. Ninety-four admissible chronology pairs were crossed with four fixed palaeolocation regions, producing 376 scenarios. The historical-persistence hypothesis used the same sign combination as the present transition result: BIO15 increase plus BIO1 decrease.

Palaeoclimate surfaces came from PALEO-PGEM (Barreto et al., 2023). We retained the fraction of chronology-by-region scenarios matching both signs and examined the central 0.79–0.74 Ma chronology separately. These fractions are deterministic sensitivity-grid fractions, not posterior probabilities.

Broader historical diagnostics tested 17 BIOCLIM variables across six dated lineage contexts and global sea-level metrics across three representative clades. These analyses constrain recurring coarse climatic or eustatic explanations; they do not reconstruct local microclimate, land bridges, biotic communities or the selective history of every trait transition.

# Results

## Three capitulum components repeatedly change within one young radiation

Thirty-six of 38 sampled Japanese concepts occur in the dominant radiation, yet multiple combinations of orientation, phyllary posture and stickiness occur within it. Orientation required four to six minimum state changes across 1,000 bootstrap topologies, phyllary posture exactly three and stickiness exactly five (Figure 1).

These counts establish repeated differentiation within the radiation. They do not by themselves establish independent origins, convergence or adaptation.

## The capitulum is assembled mosaically through evolutionary time

Paired comparison on the same 1,000 bootstrap topologies showed strong central depth ordering. Phyllary posture was deeper-permissive than stickiness in 1,000/1,000 topologies and deeper-permissive than orientation in 993/1,000. Orientation was deeper-permissive than stickiness in 905/1,000, with seven ties. The complete central ordering, phyllary < orientation < stickiness, occurred in 898/1,000 topologies. Coverage-matched masking retained the central phyllary-deeper pattern against matched medians, while strict tails overlapped.

Zero of three pairwise trait comparisons passed the robust shared-transition-localization rule. Thus the three components differ both in the evolutionary depth at which changes can lie and in the branches to which change is repeatedly assigned (Figure 2). The simplest synchronized whole-capitulum history is therefore unsupported.

## Repeated components map to plausible functional interfaces

The independent functional evidence did not support one common mechanism for all three components (Figure 3). For orientation, experimentally erect *Cremanthodium campanulatum* capitula produced fewer achenes; water and UV-B reduced pollen viability, whereas pollinator preference and meaningful orientation-driven internal thermal differences were not detected (Niu & Sun, 2013). This makes abiotic reproductive exposure, with possible timing effects, the leading current functional domain.

For phyllary architecture, removing involucral spines in *Centaurea solstitialis* removed deterrence of illegitimate Lepidoptera and reduced filled seeds by 22%, without increasing legitimate bee/fly visitation frequency (Agrawal et al., 2000). Independent protective-bract manipulation likewise supports an antagonist-access pathway. Mechanical antagonist exclusion is therefore the leading functional prior, although direct focal *Cirsium* phyllary manipulation is absent.

Stickiness showed a different pattern. Direct *Cirsium* experiments include both defence-benefit and null outcomes, and arthropod-guild observations show that pollinators avoid trap contact, several seed predators bypass sticky zones, ants and aphids are deterred and some predatory arthropods use sticky inflorescences (Willson et al., 1983; Thomas, 2003, 2007). A universal sticky-defence rule is therefore contradicted; local arthropod-community filtering under a benefit/cost trade-off is the leading model.

Across four independent *Cirsium* reproductive-herbivory programmes, experimentally reducing insect herbivory increased viable or mature seed output 2.674-fold (95% CI 2.388–2.993; I² = 1.02%). This corresponds to an estimated 62.6% loss of potential seed output under ambient herbivory. Reproductive antagonism can therefore impose a large fitness cost in the genus, although this pooled effect is not attributed to any one of the three focal traits.

## Orientation transitions track a present ecological regime

The common nine-variable screen did not recover one shared abiotic syndrome across the three traits. Orientation was weak as a static state contrast, phyllary posture was state-replication limited and stickiness showed exploratory precipitation-related leads that did not survive correction across the 27-row family.

Orientation became structured when environmental change was aligned to reconstructed trait transitions (Figure 4). The fixed upward-to-downward BIO15-up/BIO1-down composite ranked 16/792 (2.02%) for n>=5, 19/1,716 (1.11%) for n>=3 and 4/126 (3.17%) for n>=10. The reverse transition tracked the opposite side of the same strict vector, yielding a bidirectional-floor rank of 3/126 (2.38%).

Single-taxon deletion retained the U→D composite direction in 9/9 panels, while its exact <=0.05 finite-map rank survived 2/9. In the bidirectional decomposition, both forward and reverse alignments remained positive in 9/9 deletion panels and the exact <=0.05 bidirectional-floor rank survived 3/9. The result remained exceptional after latitude/longitude residualization, internal-edge-only scoring and their combination.

The Japan/Taiwan source-partition mean component was opposite to the observed direction and non-exceptional (502/792 = 63.38%), whereas the within-partition component remained positive and exceptional (20/792 = 2.53%). Preserving source-partition orientation-state counts retained the within-partition result (10/336 = 2.98%). Thus the orientation result is not reducible to a simple source-partition contrast under the declared estimator.

## The present orientation regime fails as a model of historical origin

For the sole orientation event with bounded chronology and palaeolocation, the present BIO15-up/BIO1-down regime matched only 99/376 historical scenarios (26.3%; Figure 5). Regional match fractions were 21.3% for Taiwan, 9.6% for the Ryukyu corridor, 43.6% for southern Japan and 30.9% for the East-Asian core corridor. Only 6/94 admissible chronologies matched the regime in all four regions.

The central 0.79–0.74 Ma chronology was especially informative. BIO1 decreased in all four regions, consistent with one half of the present U→D vector, but BIO15 also decreased in all four regions, opposite to the present expectation. The present two-axis regime therefore failed in 4/4 central regional scenarios.

The broader historical-cause screen reached the same boundary at coarser resolution. Across 17 BIOCLIM variables, six dated lineage contexts and 15,472 scenario × variable combinations, zero of 324 event-level climate classes passed the robust criterion. The three-clade global sea-level diagnostic likewise yielded 0/21 robust event-metric classes.

The result is not that environment was irrelevant. Rather, no recurring coarse tested climatic or global eustatic regime is identified as the historical trigger under the admitted uncertainty.
