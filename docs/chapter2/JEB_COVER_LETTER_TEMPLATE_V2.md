Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, “Modular evolutionary depth and lineage-dependent environmental correspondence of capitulum traits in a young thistle radiation,” for publication in the *Journal of Evolutionary Biology*.

The manuscript addresses a general problem in evolutionary inference: when similar phenotypes recur, present environmental sorting does not necessarily identify the environment in which the phenotype originated, and components of one complex structure need not share one evolutionary trajectory. We use a young *Cirsium* radiation to connect repeated trait history, explicit phylogenetic uncertainty, present ecological correspondence, public divergence-time constraints, palaeoenvironmental trajectories and replicated lineage comparisons while preserving the boundary between comparative evidence and adaptation.

Three findings motivate the manuscript. First, orientation, phyllary posture and involucre stickiness all require repeated minimum changes, but their count stability, relative lineage-depth and transition localization differ, and zero of three trait pairs retains a robust common transition-localization pattern. Second, orientation shows strong present hydric correspondence across independently defined spatial and East-Asian niche layers, yet a 376-scenario chronology × palaeolocation analysis leaves its historical origin environment unresolved. This demonstrates directly why present ecological sorting should not be projected backward into evolutionary history. Third, two publicly dated white–coloured sister systems independently recover a lower-chroma white phenotype, but the current radiation contrast agrees with the global radiation–colour relationship in one lineage pair and reverses in the other. The same two white systems nevertheless share several coarse head-geometry directions while finer geometry is heterogeneous.

Together these results support an intermediate model: capitulum evolution is neither one synchronized whole-structure syndrome nor complete module independence. Instead, the public evidence is best described as unequal evolutionary depth with partial coordinated remodelling and lineage-/scale-dependent environmental correspondence. The value of the study lies as much in its explicit discordances and identifiability limits as in positive associations.

The manuscript does not infer independent origins from minimum changes, adaptive convergence, exact trait-transition ages, ancestral-area probabilities, historical rain or solar causation, a common pigment/developmental mechanism or reproductive-fitness benefit. These are reserved for ancestry-linked mechanism and fitness tests. We believe this resolution-aware treatment of repeated phenotype, environmental history and phylogenetic uncertainty is broadly relevant to evolutionary biology beyond the focal taxon.
