# Three-trait common9 present-ecology result v1

## Why this analysis was run

The earlier three-trait comparison used only BIO1, BIO4, BIO12 and BIO15 because those were the frozen Chapter 2 climate variables. That was sufficient as a common first-pass climate comparison, but too narrow to support a broad ecological-interface interpretation. This analysis therefore passes orientation, phyllary posture and involucre stickiness through the same pre-existing nine-variable environment universe: BIO1, BIO4, BIO12, BIO15, RSDS, VPD, WIND, GSP and NPP.

The occurrence provenance frame, QC, spatial thinning, trait ontologies and primary >=3-occurrence gate were fixed before execution. The orientation-specific BIO15/BIO1 directional hypothesis was not transferred to phyllary or stickiness.

## Results

### Orientation

Seventeen taxa (5 D, 12 U) were evaluable. The nine-dimensional omnibus rank was 2138/6188 = 34.55%. No single common9 axis showed an exceptional static tip-state split; the strongest was GSP at 1005/6188 = 16.24%.

Therefore the ecological information in orientation is not a simple present-day D-versus-U niche split across the common Japan provenance panel. The separately frozen phylogeny-conditioned transition-level result remains the stronger ecological evidence.

### Phyllary posture

Only four primary-gate taxa were evaluable (3 ascending, 1 appressed), yielding only four possible label maps. The nine-dimensional omnibus rank was 4/4. Lowering the occurrence gate to one record increased coverage to six taxa but left appressed and spreading represented by one lineage each; the omnibus rank was 28/30 = 93.33%.

Thus adding environmental dimensions does not solve the phyllary problem. The limiting factor is replicated state coverage, not the number of abiotic variables.

### Involucre stickiness

Twelve taxa (6 sticky, 6 nonsticky) were evaluable. The nine-dimensional omnibus rank was 116/924 = 12.55%.

The strongest single-axis leads were precipitation-related and directionally coherent:

- GSP: sticky - nonsticky = +1.278 SD; 18/924 = 1.95%;
- BIO12: +1.311 SD; 22/924 = 2.38%;
- BIO15: +1.048 SD; 70/924 = 7.58%.

However, GSP and BIO12 both have BH q = 0.321 across the 27 primary trait-by-environment rows. They are therefore exploratory precipitation-related leads, not independent confirmatory discoveries.

## Cross-trait interpretation

The common9 expansion does **not** reveal one shared abiotic niche axis for the three historical capitulum traits.

Instead it clarifies why the ecological evidence should not be forced into one climate template:

1. orientation is best resolved by transition-conditioned ecological correspondence rather than a static tip-state split;
2. stickiness has a replicated present-niche panel with a coherent but multiplicity-limited precipitation lead;
3. phyllary posture remains constrained by public state replication.

This supports a manuscript design in which the three traits share one historical comparison framework but are allowed to differ in the ecological evidence appropriate to their proposed interfaces. Climate/common9 is a common abiotic screen, not a complete test of mechanical-access or arthropod-community mechanisms.

## Claim boundary

Do not claim that common9 identifies adaptation, selection, historical cause, a universal climatic driver, or the direct mechanisms of phyllary posture and stickiness. In particular, absence of a common9 signal does not test visitor access, antagonists, or arthropod-community composition directly.
