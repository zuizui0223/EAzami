# Chapter 2 public-data completion: spatial breadth × phylogenetic depth

## Current position

Chapter 2 is closed only when two independent public-data views are made commensurable without being collapsed:

- **Azami — diversity breadth:** broad present-day spatial variation in image-derived capitulum traits and their environmental correspondence;
- **EAzami — diversity depth:** topology-conditioned minimum histories, relative event-depth and phylogeny-aware ecological correspondence;
- **joint Chapter 2 result:** which ecological directions recur across scales, which are scale-specific, and which remain impossible to evaluate with public data.

The active sequence is therefore:

**spatial trait–environment structure → minimum historical change → relative event-depth → phylogeny-aware ecology → space–time concordance → own-data boundary**.

## Why precipitation amount and seasonality must be separated

Azami's spatial orientation signal and EAzami's BIO15 orientation signal concern the same broad rain-exposure domain, but annual precipitation amount (BIO12) and precipitation seasonality (BIO15) are not interchangeable. A downward/nodding capitulum could correspond to high total wetness, concentrated seasonal rain, short extreme wet periods, flowering-season rain, or a combination of these.

The next analysis therefore estimates, on the same EAzami taxon panels and public phylogenetic ensembles:

1. BIO12 ~ orientation;
2. BIO15 ~ orientation;
3. BIO15 ~ orientation + BIO12;
4. BIO12 ~ orientation + BIO15.

The partial models are small-panel diagnostics, not causal models. Their purpose is to identify whether the current temporal signal is mainly an amount axis, a seasonality axis, or an unresolved mixture. The analysis is repeated across genuine induced topology variation: regenerated concatenated UFBoot trees, public locus trees, ASTRAL and source-defined occurrence panels. The six optimized AU trees are retained as branch-length realizations but are not called six distinct ecology-panel topologies because they collapse to one induced topology after pruning.

## Flower colour and solar exposure

The colour question is split into measurable components:

- CIELAB lightness (L*);
- chroma (C*);
- pigment direction / hue components;
- within-taxon colour variation.

The preferred exposure is not an undifferentiated annual radiation value. For flowers, the hierarchy is:

1. flowering-season surface downwelling shortwave radiation;
2. UV-B or biologically effective UV if a reusable public layer can be frozen;
3. flowering-season temperature;
4. elevation and cloudiness as correlated axes.

Azami can test the spatial relationship using its public-image observations. EAzami can test the phylogeny-aware relationship only after an exact-concept, source-balanced colour panel is linked to the public phylogeny and occurrence/environment data. The existing seven-concept source-balanced lightness diagnostic remains a bounded negative/low-resolution result; it is not discretized to manufacture a fourth completed trait history.

## Space–time interpretation classes

| Class | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `multiscale_concordant` | same trait, commensurable environmental axis and direction survive spatial and phylogeny-aware analyses |
| `domain_concordant_axis_distinct` | both point to one domain, such as rain exposure, but use non-equivalent axes such as amount and seasonality |
| `scale_specific` | the relationship is evaluable at both scales but retained at only one |
| `unresolved` | direction is estimable but source, covariance or sampling uncertainty prevents a stable class |
| `not_evaluable` | trait × environment × phylogeny overlap is insufficient |

## What concordance permits us to say

Concordant spatial and phylogenetic results can support:

> **a multiscale ecological correspondence consistent with adaptive differentiation**

They cannot alone support:

> **the trait is an adaptation produced by the named environmental factor**.

The stronger statement requires an event-specific exposure match and independent function/fitness evidence. Chapter 2 can reach the former with public data; Chapter 3 supplies the latter with same-individual genomics, direct mechanism measurements and fitness tests.

## Completion gates

1. freeze the Azami spatial-effect extraction for orientation–precipitation and colour–radiation;
2. partition BIO12 and BIO15 in EAzami on all current occurrence panels and public phylogenetic ensembles;
3. exhaust source-auditable public colour observations under a source-balanced taxon-concept contract;
4. estimate flowering-season radiation/temperature rather than annual proxies where phenology is recoverable;
5. publish a machine-readable space–time concordance ledger and rewrite the manuscript around supported, unresolved and not-evaluable cross-scale claims.

The operational phrase **public-data saturation** means every machine-reusable source in the frozen registry has been queried under the same predeclared filters. It does not mean every webpage, photograph or unpublished record on the internet has been exhausted.
