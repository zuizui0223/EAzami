## Historical development now represented continuously

### 1999–2000: foundational Carduinae hypotheses

Häffner & Hellwig's ITS analysis and Häffner's morphological/anatomical monograph established the first explicit modern hypotheses for Carduinae generic affinities and the Carduus–*Cirsium* complex. These studies are historically indispensable but insufficient for rapid radiations or reticulate histories.

### 2002–2013: combined nuclear/plastid and biogeographic frameworks

Garcia-Jacas et al., Susanna et al. and Barres et al. refined tribal/subtribal limits, dated Cardueae diversification and proposed geographic histories. Their few-locus topologies remain testable historical hypotheses rather than the preferred present species backbone.

### 2014–2019: reusable target enrichment and Hyb-Seq

Mandel et al. developed Compositae target enrichment; later family- and tribe-scale work created reproducible nuclear matrices, gene trees, species trees and dating frameworks. This is the methodological lineage underlying current Compositae1061-compatible sampling.

### 2020–2026: global/regional species phylogenomics and integrative delimitation

Target capture and phylotranscriptomics now provide:

- a broad global *Cirsium* species-level tree;
- a substantial Japanese species context;
- a North American target-capture radiation;
- focal Taiwan–Ryukyu species frameworks;
- integrative Afrocarduus delimitation and biogeography;
- increasing genome, transcriptome, plastome and cytotype resources.

## What is resolved

- deep Asteraceae/Cardueae/Carduinae nuclear relationships are usable for project purposes;
- a broad but incomplete global *Cirsium* nuclear backbone exists;
- Japanese species-level relationships are substantially sampled;
- focal Sinocirsium, Arenicola and Nipponocirsium relationships are well supported at species scale;
- North America has an independent regional phylogenomic framework;
- the origin and compatibility of the Compositae target-enrichment framework are traceable through public data.

## What remains genuinely unresolved

- population placement of white and coloured morphs;
- local ancestry, introgression and standing ancestral variation;
- mixed cytotypes, homeolog sorting and paralogy;
- transition-critical nuclear gaps in China, Korea and the Russian Far East after synonym/public-tip audits;
- exact machine-readable Moreyra and Chang trees, branch lengths and complete sample provenance;
- molecular causes of independent white flowers;
- any demonstrated white-to-coloured evolutionary regain.

## Important 2026 correction: plastome coverage is no longer sparse at tribe scale

Abdullah et al. 2026 assembled or sequenced chloroplast genomes for 54 Cardueae species and combined them with 18 existing plastomes, producing a 72-species, 51-genus dataset covering all 12 subtribes.

This closes a **tribe-wide maternal-genome resource gap**, but not a nuclear species-tree gap. The study itself recovered cytonuclear disagreement, including placements conflicting with nuclear phylogenomics. EAzami therefore retains:

- nuclear species trees for primary organismal history;
- plastid trees as separate maternal histories;
- discordance as evidence for testing capture, introgression or lineage sorting.

## Important 2025–2026 comparison: repeated morphology in a young thistle radiation

The 489-locus Afrocarduus studies reconstructed a young mountain radiation and recovered two independent origins of the stemless habit. Species limits were substantially underestimated under a broad traditional concept, and at least one paraphyletic result was attributed plausibly to ILS or hybridization.

Although Afrocarduus is not the focal East Asian lineage, it provides a close methodological and evolutionary comparison:

- conspicuous thistle traits can evolve repeatedly;
- one morphology need not define one clade;
- dense nuclear loci plus morphology are needed for integrative delimitation;
- young radiations require explicit discordance analysis.

## Current flower-colour inference

The expanded evidence does not support treating *C. irumtiense* as a demonstrated regain. Under the published sister context, independent white loss in *C. brevicaule* remains the simpler current interpretation.

Bluish-purple var. *takaoense* remains the strongest regain candidate, but the following histories remain unresolved:

1. two independent white losses;
2. one shared white transition followed by coloured reactivation;
3. ancestral white/coloured polymorphism;
4. introgression of a coloured haplotype.

A true regain requires a population-aware nuclear history, local-ancestry analysis, an intact recoverable pigment pathway and a derived functional/expression-restoring change linked to phenotype in the same individuals.

## Sequencing consequence

### Species-level gaps

Use Compositae1061-compatible target capture only where the evidence audit confirms a genuine transition-critical nuclear absence. Analyse conservative single-copy, Moreyra-compatible and paralog/homeolog-aware matrices, plus separate plastid and network sensitivities.

### Population/morph histories

Use RAD-seq or resequencing for paired white/coloured populations and transregional bridge samples. Require ploidy/cytotype evidence, vouchers, standardized colour, pigment chemistry, floral RNA and leaf DNA.
