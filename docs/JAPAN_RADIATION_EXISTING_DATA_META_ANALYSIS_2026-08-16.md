# Japanese Cirsium radiation — existing-data meta-analysis ceiling

Date: 2026-08-16

## Current result

The current EAzami evidence has moved beyond a generic “rapid radiation” statement. Before the heavy 294/296 branch-length tree is available, the existing data already establish a sharply asymmetric radiation problem and expose the exact information gates that separate a rapid radiation from a demonstrated adaptive radiation.

### 1. Radiation-success asymmetry

The current Japanese-origin synthesis contains 38 sampled Japanese paper taxon concepts:

- 36 in the dominant Japanese radiation;
- 2 current exceptions;
- dominant fraction = 36/38 = 0.9474;
- dominant sampled richness / all sampled exceptions combined = 36/2 = 18.

Under the current 3-history point hypothesis the observed sampled occupancy is 36:1:1 (dominant radiation : *C. lineare* : *C. dipsacolepis*).

This is a strong descriptive meta-result but **not** a diversification-rate estimate because history ages, unsampled richness and branch lengths are not yet jointly controlled.

### 2. Replication strength of exceptional histories

- *C. lineare*: supported as exceptional in 3/3 high-dimensional analyses and 2/2 independent high-dimensional data-generation groups.
- *C. dipsacolepis*: supported in the one currently available independent high-dimensional group.
- Arenicola: not currently supported as a fourth Japanese colonization history.

Therefore *C. lineare* is the strongest comparator for asking why the dominant Japanese history radiated while another independently arrived history did not.

### 3. Recent flower-colour history is demonstrably compressed by species-tip coding

Four reviewed W/C-polymorphic systems are currently source-backed:

- var. *takaoense*;
- *C. aomorense*;
- *C. sieboldii*;
- *C. pendulum*.

All 4/4 are reduced to one taxon-level `P` state in a species-tip matrix. Direct morph↔high-dimensional nuclear linkage exists for only 1/4 systems (25%).

For var. *takaoense*, the one currently testable system:

- species-level ambiguous coding: minimum 1 transition;
- sample/population-aware W/C coding: minimum 2 transitions;
- delta = +1; minimum-count ratio = 2.0.

This directly shows under-resolution of recent state history, but it does not yet establish a replicated transition-rate increase because 0/4 systems currently permit a branch-length rate comparison.

### 4. Cytogenetic diversity is already present but sparsely sampled

The current focal Japanese cytotype audit contains five taxon-level records. Four belong to the dominant radiation and collectively include 2x, 4x and 6x states.

This establishes that the dominant radiation is not cytogenetically uniform. It does not establish how many ploidy transitions occurred, whether mixed cytotypes occur within populations, or whether cytogenetic change predicts diversification success.

### 5. Macro-trait disparity is large but not a rate estimate

The independent Azami snapshot contributes:

- 216 taxa;
- 3,725 observations;
- 6,626 heads;
- 9 image-derived endpoints.

Across endpoints, visible variance below assigned-species means remains large (0.589–0.931 in the primary nested decomposition), while the precision-aware cross-module visible-variation versus spatial-climate-association relation is near zero (`rho=-0.0569`; hierarchical effect `-0.0416`, `P=0.732`).

This supports a modular/non-universal-lability view of capitulum phenotype. It is not evidence of genetic variance, evolutionary rate or adaptation until trait values are placed on the accepted nuclear topology and measurement uncertainty is propagated.

## Existing-data analyses still possible before new field sampling

Once the accepted branch-length topology ensemble is available, public/existing data can still test:

1. age/sampling-aware radiation-success asymmetry;
2. internode compression and lineage accumulation;
3. gene-tree discordance/concordance metrics where gene trees are recoverable;
4. Azami→EAzami trait mapping for colour, orientation, outline and existing involucre/spine proxies;
5. public-occurrence niche divergence for the dominant radiation, *lineare*, *dipsacolepis* and continental sisters;
6. topology-weighted trait disparity/transition histories;
7. literature-mapped ploidy/genome-size sensitivity.

These analyses must be exhausted before expanding field sampling simply because a macro result is unresolved.

## Questions that remain non-identifiable without new biological data

New data are required for:

- replicated population-aware colour transition rates beyond var. *takaoense*;
- standing variation versus introgression in *pendulum*, *sieboldii* and Arenicola;
- population cytotype distributions and ploidy-aware local ancestry;
- direct causal links from capitulum traits to performance/fitness;
- replicated genotype → floral expression → pigment → phenotype mechanisms.

The detailed machine-readable gate matrix is `data/evidence/japan_radiation_data_requirement_gates_v1.csv`.

## Current interpretation

### Strong

- rapid Japanese radiation;
- radiation-success asymmetry as a descriptive meta-pattern;
- existence of ILS/reticulation and cytogenetic dynamism in young East-Asian *Cirsium*.

### Partial

- ecological/phenotypic differentiation over short timescales;
- modular trait divergence;
- species-tip under-resolution of recent flower-colour transitions.

### Unresolved

- elevated radiation-wide trait evolutionary rate;
- elevated niche-diversification rate;
- reticulation/ploidy as drivers of evolvability;
- strict adaptive radiation.

Until the unresolved gates are crossed, the preferred terminology is **rapid Japanese radiation**, **radiation-success asymmetry**, and **adaptive-radiation/evolvability hypothesis**, not a demonstrated adaptive radiation.
