# Capitulum multi-trait adaptive-evolution programme

Status: 2026-08-24

## Central question

> How did a young, reticulating East-Asian/Japanese *Cirsium* radiation generate large capitulum diversity so rapidly, and which ecological selection pressures repeatedly favour different capitulum trait modules?

The unit of the doctoral programme is **not head orientation**. Orientation is only the first experimentally tractable module. The programme treats the capitulum as a composite adaptive phenotype whose semi-independent modules can enter different ecological pathways.

## Azami -> EAzami transformation

`zuizui0223/azami` asks:

`what capitulum traits vary globally -> how much variation is within versus among taxa -> which traits covary with environment`

EAzami asks:

`which selection pressures act on each module -> which ecological process the module changes -> how often the module changes on ancestry -> whether the change reaches reproductive fitness`

Thus EAzami does not repeat the Azami environment-association analysis. It adds **ancestry, ecological mechanism and fitness**.

## Common analysis template for every capitulum module

For each trait/module, use the same four-step adaptive-evolution test:

1. **Selection pressure** — pollinator opportunity, reproductive assurance, antagonist pressure, rain/wetting, UV, temperature, drought, wind, density/resource limitation, or their interaction.
2. **Ecological mechanism** — effective pollination, pollen/reproductive-organ protection, antagonist access/damage, floral apparency, resource allocation, or microclimate.
3. **Evolutionary history** — population-validated trait states mapped on the nuclear history; transition number/direction/rate; repeated environmental context; standing variation/introgression/cytotype where relevant.
4. **Fitness closure** — filled achenes/viable seeds first, recruitment only where the seed-output contrast survives the demographic gate.

A trait is not called adaptive merely because it covaries with environment or changes repeatedly.

## Trait modules

### M1 — Display quantity: capitulum size and head number

**Phenotypes**
- capitulum/head diameter and/or floral display size;
- number of simultaneously open heads;
- seasonal head/flower production;
- local flowering density and neighbour display.

**Competing selection pressures**
- pollinator attraction/discovery;
- within-plant probing and possible diminishing returns/geitonogamy;
- florivore/predisposal seed-predator apparency;
- resource allocation trade-offs and local density.

**Current literature prior**
*Cirsium purpuratum* supports simultaneous pollinator benefit and predispersal seed-predator cost, with pollinator response depending on local density. Therefore the primary hypothesis is a **context-dependent benefit-cost surface**, not `larger/more heads is always fitter`.

**Evolutionary prediction**
Independent lineages/populations may evolve different display optima under different combinations of pollinator scarcity, enemy pressure and local density.

**Required fitness path**
`size/head number + density -> pollinator discovery/probing/effective contact AND antagonist discovery/damage -> filled achenes per head and per plant`.

### M2 — Orientation: erect/upward versus nodding/downward and continuous angle

**Phenotypes**
- actual capitulum angle relative to gravity;
- peduncle/branch architecture causing or constraining angle;
- true nodding versus apparent self-weight droop retained separately.

**Competing selection pressures**
- rain/wetting;
- UV exposure;
- temperature/time-window microclimate;
- pollinator effective-contact timing;
- wind/exposure where justified;
- antagonist discovery/access as a separate pathway.

**Current evolutionary result**
The current 17 resolved taxon-concept states require at least five changes on all six AU-nonrejected nuclear topologies. This is a repeated-state lower bound, not proof of parallel adaptation.

**Evolutionary prediction**
Independent orientation changes may repeatedly solve similar abiotic-protection or time-window-pollination problems, but the mechanism must be tested rather than inferred from state recurrence.

**Required fitness path**
`orientation -> time-resolved effective pollination and/or wetting/UV/thermal protection and/or antagonist exposure -> filled achenes`.

### M3 — Defensive envelope: phyllary architecture and spines

**Phenotypes**
- direct botanical phyllary posture/spread;
- spine length, direction and density;
- involucre architecture/hardness where measurable.

**Selection pressures**
- florivore and predispersal seed-predator access;
- potential pollinator handling/access cost;
- abiotic exposure only if independently supported.

**Current evidence boundary**
Azami image-derived spine-like geometry is an environmental proxy only. Broader Cardueae/Asteraceae evidence makes defence plausible, but focal *Cirsium* adaptation remains unproven.

**Evolutionary prediction**
Repeated increases in excluding architecture should occur where reproductive enemy pressure is high enough to outweigh pollinator-access/resource costs.

**Required fitness path**
`validated spine/phyllary architecture -> antagonist access/damage down +/- pollinator effective contact down -> filled achenes`.

### M4 — Stickiness / mucilage

**Phenotypes**
- direct sticky/mucilaginous state and quantitative strength where possible.

**Selection pressures**
- florivore/seed-predator exclusion or trapping;
- possible incidental costs to legitimate visitors or resource allocation.

**Current evidence boundary**
A direct *C. discolor* neutralization experiment weakens the generic `sticky inflorescence = reproductive defence` hypothesis. Stickiness therefore remains a separate low-priority module rather than being merged with spines into a generic defence score.

**Evolutionary prediction**
No general adaptive direction is assumed. The module is reopened only if Japan-wide phylogenetic/environmental mapping or focal natural variation predicts a specific repeatable function.

### M5 — Flower colour / pigmentation

**Phenotypes**
- calibrated visible hue/saturation/intensity;
- UV reflectance/absorbance where feasible;
- pigment concentration/chemistry in focal systems.

**Competing selection pressures**
- pollinator sensory environment and visitor guild;
- temperature/cold stress;
- high UV/light exposure;
- drought/oxidative stress;
- ancestry and retained/introgressed pigment machinery.

**Important claim boundary**
`colder -> darker/more pigmented` is a **testable hypothesis**, not a current *Cirsium* conclusion. Anthocyanin can have abiotic protective roles in plants, but the sign and magnitude of the capitulum-colour response must be estimated in the focal radiation and separated from pollinator/ancestry effects.

**Evolutionary hypotheses**
1. ecological: pigmentation intensity/state repeatedly shifts under different combinations of pollinator and abiotic selection;
2. historical: white/coloured states may change repeatedly within the young radiation;
3. molecular: retained/regulatory anthocyanin machinery may permit coloured re-expression after white loss.

**Required fitness path**
`ancestry-resolved colour/pigment -> effective pollination and/or floral abiotic performance -> filled achenes`.

Molecular closure for independent transitions:
`ancestry -> coding/regulatory haplotype -> floral RNA -> pigment chemistry -> calibrated colour`.

### M6 — Gross capitulum/involucre architecture

Azami shows that gross outline traits are generally weaker or more model-dependent than orientation and colour, but they should not be deleted from the adaptive programme. Retain them as an exploratory structural module and promote a specific architecture variable only if it has:

1. a direct repeatable botanical measurement;
2. a plausible ecological mechanism;
3. ancestry-aware repeated variation;
4. a discriminating fitness prediction.

Do not create a generic morphology score merely to maximize trait count.

## Cross-module evolutionary hypotheses

### H1 — Selection mosaic
Different local combinations of pollinator opportunity, reproductive assurance, enemy pressure and abiotic exposure favour different trait modules or trait combinations. There is no universal best capitulum.

### H2 — Modular evolvability
The young radiation can diversify rapidly because semi-independent developmental/genetic modules for display, orientation, defence and pigmentation can be retained, introgressed or redeployed across shallow lineages.

### H3 — Common-lability alternative
A single shared whole-capitulum lability axis may explain the same apparent multi-trait variation. Modular evolvability is supported only if module-specific transition histories and ecological fitness paths predict better than this shared-latent alternative.

### H4 — Multi-trait adaptive syndromes
Selection may act on combinations rather than one trait at a time. After single-module mechanisms are identifiable, test whether recurrent combinations (for example small display + stronger defence, or nodding + high pigmentation) represent repeatable adaptive syndromes. Do not search arbitrary trait combinations before module-specific effects are estimable.

## Multi-scale design

### Global discovery: Azami
Continuous image phenotypes and environmental structure identify candidate modules and relevant scale.

### Japan/East-Asia macroevolution: EAzami
Use common-locus nuclear phylogeny plus population-validated phenotypes to estimate repeated transitions, phylogenetic signal, correlated evolution and environmental/interaction context.

### Population microevolution
Use RADseq/resequencing selectively to estimate ancestry, standing variation, introgression and gene flow for focal polymorphic/contact systems.

### Causal ecology
Nest field manipulation/observation in ancestry-resolved populations and close trait -> mechanism -> reproductive fitness.

## Sampling consequences

Japan-wide breadth sampling must not be optimized only for orientation. The breadth panel should cover:

- geographic extremes and origin falsifiers;
- contrasting orientation states;
- extremes/contrasts in display, pigmentation, defensive architecture and stickiness where authority/voucher evidence permits;
- cytotype diversity;
- island and edaphic radiations;
- polymorphic systems that permit within-species inference.

For every new backbone individual, collect or document as many of the following as biologically available on the **same plant**:

`voucher/locality -> capitulum size -> head number/display -> actual orientation -> calibrated visible/UV colour -> direct phyllary/spine architecture -> stickiness -> DNA -> plastid -> cytotype`.

This allows the same Japan-wide backbone to support multiple trait histories rather than building a new taxon panel for each module.

## Experimental priority versus conceptual priority

Orientation remains **experimental tranche 1** because a sham/reorientation manipulation is comparatively clean and gives the fastest causal test of the general framework.

This does **not** make orientation the central doctoral trait.

Conceptual priority is multi-trait:

`display | orientation | defence envelope | stickiness | pigmentation`

all tested under the same selection-pressure -> mechanism -> evolutionary-history -> fitness architecture.

## Intended paper architecture

### Paper A — Azami global phenomics
Global continuous within/among-taxon capitulum phenotype and environmental structure. No adaptive claim.

### Paper B — Multi-trait evolutionary history of Japanese capitula
Japan-wide common-locus nuclear history + population/voucher-validated capitulum traits. Main question: did a young radiation repeatedly redeploy multiple capitulum modules, and do transition contexts differ among display, orientation, defence and colour?

This should be **multi-trait**, not an orientation-only paper.

### Papers C/D — causal module papers
Use the strongest discriminating systems emerging from Paper B. Orientation can be first because it is experimentally tractable; colour/re-expression is a likely mechanistic flagship; display and defence can become independent causal papers if their trait -> mechanism -> fitness paths are sufficiently strong.

### Thesis synthesis
Compare module-specific versus common-lability models and ask whether repeated ecological function across modules is sufficient to justify an adaptive-radiation interpretation.

## Current claim

The current evidence supports a young Japanese radiation, a general selection-mosaic prior, a large reproductive antagonist cost, display benefit-cost trade-offs, a topology-robust repeated orientation signal, and unresolved repeated colour/defence histories.

It does **not** yet establish a general adaptive capitulum syndrome or adaptive radiation. Those require multi-trait evolutionary mapping plus at least one, preferably multiple, ancestry-resolved trait -> ecological mechanism -> reproductive-fitness demonstrations.
