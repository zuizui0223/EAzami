# Aim 2 quantitative meta-analysis v2 — seed-output cost of insect herbivory

## Estimand

`RR = mean viable/mature seed output under experimentally reduced insect herbivory / mean seed output under ambient herbivory`

This analysis uses only contrasts with directly reported seed-output means and SEs. Multiple habitats, apical-damage strata, or years from one data-generation study are collapsed before across-study pooling.

## Coverage

- 9 within-study contrasts
- 4 independent data-generation studies
- *Cirsium canescens* and *C. occidentale* var. *occidentale*

Studies span Louda & Potvin 1995, Maron et al. 2002, West & Louda 2018, and Russell et al. 2025. The 2025 experiment used different monocarpic adults across 2017–2019 and five Colorado sites.

## Result

Random-effects model on ln response ratio:

- pooled response ratio = **2.674**
- 95% CI = **2.388–2.993**
- equivalent interpretation: ambient insect herbivory reduces potential viable/mature seed output by about **62.6%** on average
- 95% CI for this reduction = **58.1–66.6%**
- `I² = 1.0%`

Study-level response ratios after within-study collapse:

- Louda & Potvin: **2.29**
- Maron et al.: **3.57**
- West & Louda: **2.48**
- Russell et al. 2025: **2.67**

Leave-one-study-out pooled RR stays between **2.60 and 2.73**, with every 95% interval above 1.

## Biological conclusion

This is no longer only an evidence map. Across the directly harmonizable experimental *Cirsium* literature, insect attack on reproductive structures imposes a large and highly repeatable maternal-fitness cost measured as viable/mature seed output.

That result supports the antagonist side of the proposed capitulum trade-off:

`pollinator-facing display/access benefit <-> exposure to floral/seed antagonists`

It does **not** identify which capitulum trait causes protection. Orientation, flower colour, and validated phyllary/spine architecture still require trait-specific experiments.

## Important boundary

The low `I²` applies to this narrowly harmonized seed-output estimand, not to all plant–insect interactions. Pollinator visitation, florivory incidence, recruitment, population growth, and different structural traits must not be pooled into the same response.

Older Louda/Potvin numerical means and SEs are extracted from the comparative table in Maron et al. 2002, which reports the independent Platte-thistle experiment alongside the coastal-dune experiment; provenance is kept explicit in the effect table.
