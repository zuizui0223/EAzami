# Cirsium molecular → macro flower-colour bridge — 2026-08-15

## Purpose

Identify what the existing *Cirsium* molecular literature actually establishes about the flavonoid/anthocyanin system, then ask which molecular layer remains unresolved when EAzami scales from tissue biochemistry to repeated white/coloured transitions across lineages.

This document separates:

- **published molecular conclusions**;
- **EAzami cross-source coverage result**;
- **new problem exposed by that coverage result**;
- **predictions for HMM1/HMM5**.

A gene not named in a targeted paper panel is never coded as absent from the genome or transcriptome.

## Published conclusion 1 — Roy et al. 2018

DOI: `10.5808/GI.2018.16.4.e34`.

The flower/leaf/root RNA-seq study assembled **51,133 unigenes** and explicitly reported silymarin-precursor pathway genes in its Supplementary Table 2. The displayed table directly contains, among others:

- PAL;
- C4H;
- 4CL;
- CHS;
- CHI;
- F3H;
- a flavonoid hydroxylase annotated as F3'H.

This establishes a recoverable upstream/core flavonoid molecular resource in *C. japonicum* var. *spinossimum*. The study was designed around taxifolin/coniferyl alcohol and silymarin biosynthesis, not historical white↔coloured evolution.

## Published conclusion 2 — Park et al. 2020

DOI: `10.1021/acsomega.0c04001`.

The integrated transcriptome–metabolome study reports:

- **29 orthologs** assigned to phenylpropanoid biosynthesis;
- **75 metabolites**;
- a named candidate-gene set including `PAL`, `C4H`, `4CL`, `CHS`, `CHI`, `F3H`, `F3'H`, `FSII`, `FLS`, `DFR`, `HCT`, `C3H`, `HQT`, and `COMT` families;
- most assayed flavonoid biosynthetic genes at **2.6–500×** higher expression in flowers than leaves.

Reported qRT-PCR examples include:

- `CjCHS1`: 500× flower vs leaf;
- `CjCHS2`: 112×;
- `CjF3'H`: 16.6×;
- `CjCHI`: 13×;
- `CjFLS`: 5.6×;
- `CjFSII`: 3.3×.

Two `CjDFR` isoforms are explicitly reported, although the cited contrast is root vs leaf rather than a white/coloured floral comparison.

Therefore the correct starting point is not “does *Cirsium* possess a flavonoid pathway?” The pathway is already strongly anchored. The unresolved question is which molecular layer produces repeated white/coloured switching.

## EAzami 17-family audit panel

For a flower-colour evolutionary bridge, EAzami predeclares the following comparison layers:

1. **entry** — PAL, C4H, 4CL;
2. **flavonoid core** — CHS, CHI, F3H, F3'H;
3. **branch competition** — FLS, FSII;
4. **anthocyanin committed** — DFR;
5. **anthocyanin terminal** — ANS/LDOX, UFGT/3GT;
6. **MBW regulation** — MYB, bHLH, WD40;
7. **transport/sequestration** — GST, MATE.

The first four layers test whether the biochemical route is present and how branch flux can change. The last three are especially informative for a reversible white↔coloured switch because they can alter pigment production/accumulation without requiring loss of the entire upstream pathway.

## EAzami meta-analysis result

Across the two published targeted resources:

- entry + flavonoid core are directly documented in both studies;
- Park 2020 additionally directly anchors FLS/FSII and DFR;
- the named targeted panels do not provide comparable direct coverage of ANS/UFGT, MBW regulatory components, or anthocyanin transport/sequestration components.

This is **not** evidence that ANS/UFGT/MYB/bHLH/WD40/GST/MATE are missing. Both studies generated broader transcriptomic resources than their targeted pathway tables. It is a **resolution gap between the molecular layer most densely documented and the molecular layer most diagnostic of the evolutionary colour switch**.

## New EAzami problem — P_MICRO_MACRO_07

> Existing *Cirsium* evidence strongly establishes upstream/core flavonoid machinery and floral tissue bias, but the cross-lineage evolutionary question is concentrated at a terminal/regulatory/transport layer that is not comparably resolved in the named published panels.

This problem is generated by EAzami's cross-source coverage audit. It is not copied from a source paper's future-work section.

## Consequence for HMM1

HMM1 predicts regulatory reversibility of recent white states.

The molecular bridge sharpens that prediction:

> If recent white lineages suppress a retained pathway, upstream/core genes should usually remain recoverable across W/C systems, whereas the strongest W/C-associated divergence should concentrate in DFR→ANS/UFGT, MBW regulation, transport/sequestration, or their cis-regulatory control rather than requiring repeated destruction of PAL→F3H/F3'H machinery.

A direct falsifier remains repeated irreversible disruption of indispensable pathway genes in multiple independently resolved recent white lineages.

## Consequence for HMM5

HMM5 predicts phenotype/pathway-level convergence with molecular heterogeneity.

The comparison should therefore be hierarchical:

1. phenotype — white vs coloured;
2. pathway — anthocyanin output suppressed/restored;
3. module — biosynthetic terminal / MBW / transport;
4. gene — e.g. ANS vs UFGT vs MYB;
5. nucleotide/structural variant.

Independent white systems can count as molecularly heterogeneous even when they converge on the same pathway/module.

## Claim boundary

- leaf RNA non-expression = not observed in that assay, not deletion;
- not listed in a targeted pathway table = not targeted/reported, not absence;
- coding divergence = candidate mechanism, not causal proof;
- gene-tree affinity to a colour state = ancestry evidence, not expression evidence;
- tissue-biased expression in ordinary *C. japonicum* = pathway context, not historical proof of a white-loss or regain event.
