# Chapter 2 research plan — repeated flower-colour transitions in East Asian Cirsium

## Central problem

East Asian *Cirsium* contains repeated white, pink and purple flower states, including white/coloured sister lineages and within-lineage colour polymorphism. The project asks whether floral anthocyanin pigmentation has been repeatedly lost and, in some lineages, regained or reactivated, and whether repeated transitions use homologous molecular mechanisms.

A second linked objective is to improve the nuclear phylogenetic framework required to test these transitions. Existing phylogenomic studies are used as starting backbones, but the project explicitly identifies taxa/populations whose absence or weak placement prevents reliable ancestral-state reconstruction. These gaps become priorities for RAD-seq.

Recent phylotranscriptomic work already resolves important local backbones. Chang et al. (2026; DOI 10.1186/s12870-026-08097-6) analysed 12 *Cirsium* taxa and 33 *Cirsium* samples across the *C. japonicum* complex, Arenicola and selected related taxa. Chang et al. (2025; DOI 10.1186/s40529-025-00454-2) analysed seven *Cirsium* species in Nipponocirsium/related sampling and documented strong ploidy/chromosome-number structure. Therefore RAD-seq is not justified as a simple repeat of those species placements. It is justified to (i) fill the much larger Japan–China regional taxon gap, (ii) add population replication around flower-colour transitions, and (iii) test reticulation/ploidy-sensitive histories that a single plastid tree cannot resolve.

## Aim 1 — Build a population-aware East Asian flower-colour atlas

Scope: Japan, Ryukyu Islands, Taiwan and China.

For each evidence record retain accepted taxon/source name, population/locality, country/region, individual-level colour state where possible, continuous image/spectral colour where available, evidence type/source, direct versus literature-reported colour and confidence/assessability.

Primary output: a vetted atlas that distinguishes fixed white, fixed coloured and polymorphic taxa/populations.

## Aim 2 — Audit the existing phylogenetic framework and build a targeted nuclear RAD-seq panel

For every accepted taxon and transition-critical population, record representation in published nuclear phylogenomic trees, plastid-only representation, support/resolution, nuclear–plastid conflict, ploidy, suspected hybridization/reticulation and whether missing placement changes a colour-history hypothesis.

RAD-seq priorities follow three tiers: Tier A directly distinguishes alternative loss/regain histories; Tier B fills major East Asian nuclear-backbone gaps or unstable placements; Tier C adds lower-marginal-information replication.

Population-level sampling remains important for *C. brevicaule*, *C. irumtiense*, *C. japonicum* var. *takaoense* and var. *albescens*. Nuclear analyses should remain ploidy-aware and include network/gene-flow alternatives rather than forcing all histories into one bifurcating tree.

## Aim 3 — Reconstruct repeated loss and candidate regain

Map population-aware colour states onto a topology set rather than one fixed tree. Test independent white origins, coloured↔white transitions, candidate white ancestors/intermediates, sensitivity to uncertain nodes/missing taxa/reticulation and ancestral polymorphism or introgression as alternatives to simple regain.

A candidate `regain` must survive phylogenetic sensitivity analysis. Molecular `reactivation` additionally requires evidence that the anthocyanin pathway was retained but suppressed in the white lineage and restored in a descendant coloured lineage.

## Aim 4 — Identify molecular mechanisms of replicated transitions

Select informative systems after the history analysis. For the same individuals where possible collect standardized flower colour, anthocyanin/flavonoid chemistry, floral RNA, high-quality leaf DNA and vouchers. Questions include structural-gene loss versus regulatory suppression, repeated regulatory nodes, cis/trans change, retained ancestral haplotypes and introgressed pigmentation alleles.

## Aim 5 — Selection is downstream, not assumed

Pollinator preference, UV, temperature, drought, herbivory and pigment-production costs are candidate selective agents. They are not used to infer evolutionary direction without explicit phenotype/genotype/fitness linkage.

## Legacy hypotheses retained as downstream tests

- repeated anthocyanin loss may have multiple independent origins;
- candidate regain/reactivation requires topology-sensitive and molecular support;
- repeated white-flower evolution may involve regulatory suppression more often than structural loss;
- some apparent transitions may instead reflect ancestral polymorphism or introgression.

## Current role after Chapter 2 reorganization

These questions now sit downstream of the Chapter 2 inverse-problem result. They become candidate realized-history and molecular-mechanism tests only after the present phenotypic field has been defined and the admissible-history set has been narrowed.
