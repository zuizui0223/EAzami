# Confirmed and high-priority flower-colour transition systems

Date: 2026-08-09

This note separates source-supported colour contrasts from hypothesized evolutionary direction. `Loss`, `regain`, and `reactivation` are not assigned solely from tip colours.

## System 1 — Ryukyu Arenicola

- *Cirsium brevicaule*: white.
- *Cirsium irumtiense*: coloured / bluish-purple in Chang et al. 2026.
- Nuclear relationship: both are supported as distinct Arenicola lineages in Chang et al. 2026.

Why it matters:

- classic white-versus-coloured sister-lineage contrast;
- evolutionary direction is not resolved by the tip states alone;
- population RAD-seq should focus on within-species structure and gene flow, not on re-proving species separation.

Decision target:

Distinguish ancestral coloured + white loss in *C. brevicaule* from a white intermediate followed by coloured regain in *C. irumtiense*, while testing ancestral polymorphism and introgression as alternatives.

## System 2 — Taiwanese Sinocirsium / *C. japonicum* complex

Source-supported states from Chang et al. 2026:

- var. *albescens*: white;
- var. *takaoense*: white and bluish-purple morphs within one supported lineage;
- var. *australe*: bluish-purple;
- var. *fukienense*: coloured/variable;
- Japanese var. *japonicum*: phylogenetic member of the five-lineage complex; colour evidence must be populated separately.

Nuclear relationship:

- var. *albescens* sister to var. *takaoense*;
- var. *australe* sister to var. *fukienense*;
- reticulation / ILS or historical introgression is reported in the Taiwanese complex.

Why it matters:

This system contains both a fixed-white lineage and within-lineage white/coloured polymorphism. It is the strongest current system for mapping colour-associated alleles and testing whether fixed white and polymorphic white use the same molecular route.

## System 3 — Taiwanese Nipponocirsium

Sources: Chang et al. 2019 for *C. tatakaense* description; Chang et al. 2025 for nuclear phylotranscriptomics and the description of *C. pengii*.

Source-supported states:

- *C. pengii*: bluish-purple;
- *C. kawakamii*: white;
- *C. tatakaense*: bluish-purple / purplish-red.

Nuclear relationship in Chang et al. 2025:

- Taiwanese clade is distinct from the sampled Japanese Nipponocirsium clade;
- *C. pengii* is basal within the Taiwanese clade;
- *C. kawakamii* and *C. tatakaense* diverged more recently;
- weak gene-flow signals involving *C. kawakamii* and the other Taiwanese taxa were reported.

Chromosome context:

- *C. pengii*: 2n = 2x = 32;
- *C. kawakamii*: 2n = 4x = 64;
- *C. tatakaense*: 2n = 4x = 64.

Why it matters:

This is a second white-flower system independent of the *C. japonicum* complex and Arenicola. Because the white species is embedded in a young, well-resolved nuclear clade containing coloured relatives, it is immediately useful for testing repeated loss. Its ploidy history also makes it a valuable stress test for RAD-seq and allele/homeolog handling.

Important caution:

The current topology does not by itself prove a coloured -> white transition. Ancestral-state inference must include the sampled Japanese Nipponocirsium and appropriate outgroups, and should be repeated over the tree distribution / network alternatives.

## Current priority for molecular follow-up

1. var. *takaoense* white vs coloured individuals/populations — within-lineage mapping and RNA/pigment comparison.
2. *C. kawakamii* vs coloured *C. tatakaense* plus basal *C. pengii* — replicated white-flower system with nuclear backbone already available.
3. *C. brevicaule* vs *C. irumtiense* — population history and direction of the Ryukyu contrast.
4. var. *albescens* — fixed-white comparison against white *takaoense* and the independent white systems above.

## What would count as repeated white-flower evolution

At minimum, an independent white origin requires a topology-sensitive ancestral-state analysis in which the white state arises on separate branches with adequate support. Similar terminal white phenotypes in Arenicola, Sinocirsium, and Nipponocirsium are therefore treated as independent-origin candidates until the broader East Asian topology and missing taxa are audited.

## What would count as regain/reactivation

A coloured regain candidate must satisfy all of the following:

1. a coloured lineage descends from a white ancestor/intermediate with meaningful support across topology sensitivity analyses;
2. ancestral polymorphism and introgression are explicitly tested as alternatives;
3. the white lineage retains a functional anthocyanin pathway or identifiable ancestral functional capacity;
4. the derived coloured lineage shows restoration/reactivation of that capacity through expression, regulatory or haplotypic evidence.
