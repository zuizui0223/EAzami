# Japanese within-species white-flower candidates

Date: 2026-08-09

This note records source-backed Japanese Cirsium systems where white flowers occur within a species that otherwise has coloured flowers. These are especially valuable because within-species comparisons can separate colour-associated genomic differences from broad species divergence.

## 1. Cirsium pendulum — highest-priority Japanese candidate

Evidence:

- National Museum of Nature and Science (Japan) recognizes a white-flowered form, シロバナタカアザミ, within Cirsium pendulum.
- Noda City documents natural white-flowered populations in riverine habitats and states that the white form differs from ordinary purple-flowered C. pendulum not only in flower colour but also in flowering season.
- C. pendulum occurs across northern/eastern Japan and extends to Korea, northeastern China and Siberia, making it a potentially useful continental-island bridge taxon.

Why it matters:

- natural within-species white/coloured contrast;
- broad geographic range allows repeated-origin versus single-origin tests;
- phenology difference creates a possible pleiotropy/linkage question around the colour transition;
- suitable for population RAD-seq followed by floral RNA-seq and pigment chemistry.

Initial sampling logic:

1. multiple white populations from the Kanto river systems;
2. nearby ordinary purple populations;
3. northern Japanese purple populations;
4. if feasible, continental C. pendulum samples from Korea / NE China as outgroup-like geographic context.

## 2. Cirsium sieboldii — second Japanese candidate

Evidence:

- National Museum of Nature and Science explicitly records a white-flowered form, シロバナキセルアザミ, within Cirsium sieboldii.

Why it matters:

- another independent within-species white state in the Japanese radiation;
- useful replication if its white form is genetically stable rather than a rare somatic/developmental variant.

Before dense sequencing:

- confirm herbarium/locality records for white individuals;
- quantify whether white plants form populations or isolated individuals;
- verify whether C. sieboldii is already represented in Moreyra et al. 2025 target-capture data.

## 3. Candidate classification rule

A named white form is not automatically interpreted as an independent evolutionary transition. Each candidate must pass:

- repeated natural occurrence;
- taxonomic identity confirmation;
- population-level colour documentation;
- nuclear placement / population structure;
- exclusion of horticultural origin where relevant.

These Japanese within-species systems complement Taiwanese var. takaoense and can test whether the same anthocyanin regulatory route repeatedly generates white flowers inside otherwise coloured Cirsium lineages.
