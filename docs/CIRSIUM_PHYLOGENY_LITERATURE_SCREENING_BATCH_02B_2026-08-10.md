# *Cirsium* phylogeny literature screening — batch 02B

Date: 2026-08-10

## Records added

### Barres et al. 2013 — Tier B

**Title:** Reconstructing the evolution and biogeographic history of tribe Cardueae (Compositae)  
**DOI:** `10.3732/ajb.1200058`

This study combined nuclear ribosomal and chloroplast evidence with divergence-time and ancestral-area analyses. It represents the principal broad temporal/biogeographic framework immediately before the Hyb-Seq era.

It remains useful for:

- identifying historical hypotheses tested by later phylogenomics;
- comparing inferred centres of origin and dispersal routes;
- understanding why early few-locus topologies should not be used uncritically for current character reconstruction.

It is not promoted to Tier A because its locus sampling is much smaller than modern target-capture datasets and it is not designed to resolve recent internal *Cirsium* radiations.

### Bae et al. 2015 — Tier C

**Title:** Genetic Relationship of Some *Cirsium* Plants of Korea  
**DOI:** `10.5352/JLS.2015.25.2.243`

The study sequenced the linked nuclear-ribosomal cistron spanning 18S, ITS1, 5.8S, ITS2 and partial 28S. It sampled *C. japonicum* from eight Korean localities and single accessions of *C. chanroenicum*, *C. nipponicum* and *C. schantarense*, together with GenBank comparisons.

The especially relevant observation for EAzami is that:

- *C. schantarense* has nodding capitula;
- *C. japonicum* has upward-facing capitula;
- nevertheless, their ITS-based placement was close in the reported neighbor-joining tree.

This does **not** prove repeated evolution of capitulum orientation. It shows that a conspicuous morphological contrast need not correspond to deep separation in one linked-rDNA genealogy, strengthening the need for dense nuclear loci, population sampling and explicit trait mapping.

It remains Tier C because:

- ITS is one linked multicopy locus family;
- most non-*japonicum* taxa have one sampled individual;
- the analysis used a neighbor-joining framework;
- it cannot distinguish recent divergence, incomplete lineage sorting, hybridization or concerted evolution.

## Interpretation

These additions fill two specific historical gaps without changing the current main conclusion.

1. Barres et al. explains the transition from early biogeographic few-locus hypotheses to modern Cardueae phylogenomics.
2. Bae et al. adds actual Korean nuclear evidence rather than relying only on plastomes, cytology and taxonomy.

Korea still lacks a recovered dense, common-locus, multi-species nuclear framework linked to colour states, cytotypes and vouchers. Bae et al. is therefore historical nuclear evidence, not proof that the modern Korean species backbone is complete.
