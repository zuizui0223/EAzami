# Population history in *Cirsium*: implications for testing flower-colour loss and regain

Date: 2026-08-10

## Why this evidence is kept separate from the species-tree registry

A species-level phylogeny answers where sampled lineages sit relative to one another. It does not, by itself, establish:

- whether a white or coloured allele arose once or repeatedly;
- whether the allele is ancestral standing variation;
- whether it moved by introgression;
- whether a geographically derived population represents one or multiple colonization histories;
- whether expression differences are causal, plastic or ancestry-associated.

For that reason, population-history and landscape-genetic studies are stored in `data/evidence/cirsium_population_history_literature_2026-08-10.csv` rather than counted as equivalent species-tree evidence.

## 1. Present-day ecology does not reliably predict genetic geography

Jump et al. (2003) compared *C. acaule*, *C. heterophyllum* and *C. arvense* along geographic range gradients. *C. acaule* showed declining diversity and stronger differentiation near the range edge, whereas *C. heterophyllum* did not, despite similar declines in reproduction and population isolation.

**Implication:** a visually or ecologically similar set of populations cannot be assumed to share the same demographic history. For East Asian colour morphs, geographic breadth and population replication are required even when white populations occupy similar habitats.

## 2. Admixture can preserve diversity and mimic a derived evolutionary response

Guggisberg et al. (2012) sampled 1,522 *C. arvense* individuals from 58 European and North American populations. The North American range reflected multiple introductions from divergent European sources. A bottleneck occurred, but admixture retained substantial genetic diversity.

Nobarinezhad et al. (2020) found two genetic groups and admixed populations within only 75 km in the introduced range, while measured environmental and geographic variables explained little of the structure.

**Implication:** a coloured haplotype inside a predominantly white lineage could reflect ancestry imported from another population or lineage rather than reactivation. Population structure, demographic models and local ancestry must precede a molecular regain claim.

## 3. Historical corridors and current distance are not interchangeable

Fant et al. (2014) used seven microsatellites across 24 *C. pitcheri* populations. Genetic structure reflected postglacial history and present/historical landscape connectivity. Present straight-line distance alone did not capture the full colonization history.

**Implication:** bridge populations for *C. pendulum*, *C. sieboldii*, *C. schantarense* and *C. vlassovianum* should follow plausible biogeographic corridors across Japan, Korea, northeastern China and the Russian Far East—not simply the nearest currently occupied sites.

## 4. Gene expression divergence must be interpreted after ancestry is known

Guggisberg et al. (2013) compared ten native and introduced *C. arvense* populations under control, nutrient-deficiency and shade treatments. Thousands of expressed sequence tags differed by range or showed treatment-by-range effects, and expression patterns were consistent with the two inferred North American introduction histories.

**Implication:** floral RNA-seq differences between white and coloured populations can be caused by regulatory evolution, population ancestry, environment, developmental stage or their interaction. RNA-seq therefore must be linked to the same individuals used for genotype, colour and pigment measurements, with population structure included in the model.

## 5. Cytotype and introgression can decouple morphology from a species-tree tip

Michálková et al. (2023) used AFLP, STRUCTURE, flow cytometry, chromosomes and morphometrics to confirm recurrent hybridization involving *C. bertolonii*. The study also corrected the focal species' chromosome number.

Together with broader *Cirsium* hybrid and allopolyploid evidence, this means that neither morphology nor an accepted species name guarantees a simple tree-like genomic history.

**Implication:** every focal colour system requires:

- ploidy/cytotype verification;
- species-wide structure;
- explicit gene-flow tests;
- local ancestry around candidate colour regions;
- separate plastid maternal history.

## 6. What the existing population literature can and cannot do

### It supports

- the necessity of population replication;
- demographic and colonization alternatives;
- direct tests of admixture and geographic structure;
- combining genetic, environmental and expression data;
- treating clonal growth and repeated sampling carefully;
- distinguishing current landscape from historical connectivity.

### It does not yet provide for the focal East Asian systems

- dense white/coloured var. *takaoense* genomics;
- species-wide *C. brevicaule*–*C. irumtiense* population history;
- Japanese/continental *C. pendulum* and *C. sieboldii* genomic bridges;
- colour-associated local ancestry in *C. kawakamii*;
- verified Korean white-morph population samples;
- causal anthocyanin variants or regulatory restoration.

The search has not yet recovered an East Asian *Cirsium* study that combines a modern population-genomic dataset, population-aware flower-colour phenotypes, cytotypes and candidate anthocyanin regions across the focal transitions. That remains a genuine research opportunity, subject to continued systematic search and exact taxon/synonym screening.

## 7. Required analysis sequence for a candidate regain

For each candidate coloured regain, test models in this order:

1. **Population structure:** PCA/ordination, ancestry and kinship using neutral genome-wide markers.
2. **Species/population history:** coalescent or demographic alternatives for divergence and migration.
3. **Genome-wide gene flow:** D-statistics, f-statistics or equivalent topology tests where taxon configuration permits.
4. **Local ancestry:** ask whether the colour-associated region has a different genealogy from the genomic background.
5. **Haplotype age and sharing:** distinguish new restoration from retained ancestral coloured variation.
6. **Molecular function:** pigment chemistry, floral expression, coding/regulatory variation and allele-specific expression.
7. **Functional restoration criterion:** show that a derived change restores anthocyanin production on a genomically supported white ancestral background.

## 8. Sampling consequences

### Within-lineage colour polymorphisms

For var. *takaoense*, *C. pendulum* and *C. sieboldii*:

- sample white and coloured individuals from more than one population;
- include sympatric/parapatric comparisons where possible;
- include geographically matched controls;
- avoid treating one white locality as the state of an entire taxon;
- preserve flower, RNA, pigment and DNA from the same plant.

### Transregional bridge systems

For broad northeast Asian species:

- sample across Japan, Korea, northeastern China and the Russian Far East as distribution permits;
- include historical-corridor alternatives;
- record cytotype and clonality;
- use RAD-seq/resequencing for population history only after species identity and nuclear placement are verified.

## Bottom line

The population literature makes the criterion for evolutionary reactivation stricter and more useful:

> A coloured phenotype nested near white lineages is not evidence of regain until ancestry, admixture, standing variation and cytotype history have been evaluated. Species phylogeny identifies candidate branches; population genomics determines where the colour allele came from; pigment and expression analyses determine what molecular change it does.
