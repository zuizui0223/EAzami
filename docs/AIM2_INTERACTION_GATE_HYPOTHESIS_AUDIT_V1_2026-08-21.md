# Aim 2 interaction-gate hypothesis audit v1

## Why this audit exists

The current EAzami evidence no longer supports treating climate, pollination and antagonism as three interchangeable additive predictors. Two meta-synthesis layers now show that:

1. reproductive insect antagonism has a large and repeatable direct fecundity cost;
2. the population consequence of that cost can be transmitted, context-dependent or blocked downstream;
3. animal pollination can be important even when open flowers are not currently pollen limited.

The next question is therefore not simply `which driver is strongest?`, but **where in the life cycle each driver is gated, buffered or partitioned, and which capitulum module changes that gate**.

This document converts that question into six competing / nested hypotheses and predeclares the smallest empirical tests that can distinguish them.

## Current hypothesis decisions

### HGA0 — direct abiotic scaling — WEAKENED

Claim: broad productivity, fertility, elevation or similar gradients should directly and monotonically scale the fitness effect of floral interactions.

Current contradictions:
- C. altissimum productivity did not consistently moderate herbivore demographic effect;
- fertilization moderated the herbivore effect in only a minority of transitions;
- C. canescens elevation changed phenology but did not consistently change damage, seed production or recruitment;
- environment-only structural models reproduce the joint target registry substantially worse than full interaction models.

This does **not** mean abiotic environment is unimportant. It weakens only the simple model in which broad abiotic gradients directly and monotonically determine the net interaction effect.

### HGA1 — demographic gate / bottleneck switching — WORKING SUPPORT

Claim: environmental context often acts after fecundity, at the seed-to-recruitment or density-dependent population bottleneck.

Predicted pattern:
- similar seed effects can produce different recruitment/population effects depending on safe sites, disturbance and density dependence;
- when recruitment is seed limited, fecundity differences propagate;
- when recruitment is microsite limited, fecundity differences can be strongly buffered.

Current Cirsium evidence provides repeated direct support.

**Decisive focal test:** only after a clear seed-output effect is detected, run a small seed-addition / microsite pilot rather than adding this burden to every core190 plant.

Canonical conditional template:
`sampling/aim2_seed_recruitment_gate_ledger_v1.csv`

Recommended pilot structure:
- choose 1–2 focal populations with a strong experimentally measured seed-output contrast;
- create paired microsites spanning at minimum open/bare versus intact/litter-covered recruitment opportunity;
- add a fixed seed number from a documented source batch;
- record emergence and early survival;
- test `seed treatment × microsite context`, not a broad climate correlation.

This is a conditional Gate 2 extension, not a fourth doctoral Aim and not a new core population requirement.

### HGA2 — reproductive-assurance buffering — WORKING SUPPORT

Claim: pollinator dependence and realized pollen limitation are different quantities. Autonomous selfing, pollen-donor spacing, flowering overlap and local density can buffer or expose pollination limitation.

A plant can therefore be strongly dependent on animal pollination in a bagging experiment while receiving enough pollen under current open conditions that hand supplementation gives little additional seed output.

**Decisive focal test:** separate three quantities in the same population:
1. autonomous assurance: bagged versus open;
2. current pollen deficit: supplemented-open versus ambient-open;
3. realized interaction: visits / effective contacts and local flowering context.

The existing observation-bout ledger already contains local density, flowering display, colour availability, visits and effective contacts. The new factorial assignment ledger stores only treatment assignment and links outcomes back to the canonical focal-head ledger.

Canonical treatment template:
`sampling/aim2_assurance_antagonist_factorial_ledger_v1.csv`

### HGA3 — conditional antagonist priority — NEW CANDIDATE, NOT IDENTIFIED

Claim: when pollination service is above an assurance threshold, the marginal fitness return from additional attraction can be small while reproductive-antagonist costs remain substantial. Pollinator-facing trait benefits should become stronger again when assurance falls below that threshold.

This hypothesis is generated by combining two results that are currently on different estimand scales:
- reproductive antagonism has a large, repeatable seed-output cost;
- pollinator dependence is often high, but current open pollen limitation can be absent or context dependent.

Therefore the hypothesis **cannot** be declared supported by comparing existing effect magnitudes.

**Decisive experiment: same population, same fitness scale.**

Use a blocked factorial design where feasible:

`pollination: ambient vs hand-supplemented`

×

`post-anthesis antagonist exposure: ambient vs protected`

Key design safeguards:
- randomize treatments among capitula within the same plant where enough heads exist; otherwise use matched plants and retain plant as the biological unit/block;
- use post-anthesis antagonist protection only after the pollination phase so the protection treatment does not itself exclude pollinators;
- include a sham-protection control whenever the protection structure could change microclimate;
- final outcomes remain `total_achenes`, `filled_achenes`, and seed mass in `aim2_capitulum_field_ledger_v1.csv`;
- do not compare raw visitation effect sizes to herbivory RR.

Predictions:
- high-assurance context: supplementation effect near zero, antagonist protection positive;
- low-assurance context: supplementation benefit increases;
- the crossover should be predicted by donor spacing, flowering overlap, local density or effective-contact rate rather than by broad climate alone.

### HGA4 — interaction partitioning / trade-off mitigation — NEW MECHANISTIC CANDIDATE

Claim: floral modules need not solve mutualist-antagonist conflict by sitting at a static morphological compromise. They can **partition exposure** so that beneficial and costly interactions occur in different time windows, spatial contexts or sensory channels.

Existing patterns motivating the hypothesis:
- C. arvense scent emission is temporally coupled to pollinator activity and reduced during florivore activity;
- external Asteraceae orientation evidence shows early/time-window benefits without a corresponding all-day visitation increase;
- nodding can improve achene set without static pollinator preference;
- larger floral display can simultaneously increase pollinator probing and seed-predator exposure, creating the conflict that partitioning could mitigate.

**Decisive Cirsium test:** show that a module changes the timing or efficiency of interactions without simply raising total exposure.

For orientation, the current ledger is already sufficient:
- early / mid / late time-window bouts;
- head temperature and wetting;
- visits and effective contacts separately;
- antagonist visits/damage in the same bouts;
- final achene fitness.

Support would look like:
`orientation -> shifted timing or effective-contact efficiency / reduced damaging exposure -> fitness`
while total all-day visitation may remain similar.

This is stronger than asking whether one orientation is simply preferred by pollinators.

### HGA5 — multi-stage modularity — UNRESOLVED

Claim: orientation, colour, display and validated involucre/spine architecture act on different ecological gates and vary semi-independently.

Evidence in favor:
- weak cross-module coupling in the global Azami image-phenomics layer;
- module-specific model families rank well in one structural screen;
- mechanism reductions map orientation, colour and display to different interaction pathways.

Evidence against premature closure:
- a common-lability full model nearly matches the modular model in the robust held-out screen and generated the only 11/11 core draw in that run.

Therefore modularity remains the central hypothesis, not a recovered conclusion.

**Decisive analysis after field data:** ancestry-controlled multivariate models comparing:
1. one shared latent response / random-effect structure across modules;
2. module-specific responses and covariance;
3. module-specific `trait -> interaction/protection -> seed fitness` mediation paths.

A module-specific model must improve out-of-sample prediction or explanatory fit without simply adding unpooled context parameters.

## New causal picture

The current evidence suggests a life-cycle gate architecture rather than one flat driver model:

`abiotic / local context`

→ `pollination assurance and interaction timing`

→ `effective pollen delivery / reproductive antagonism`

→ `seed output`

→ `safe-site / disturbance / density gate`

→ `recruitment / population consequence`

Different capitulum traits may enter at different arrows.

This architecture generates two new predictions that were not explicit in the original doctoral plan:

1. **threshold prediction:** the same attractiveness trait can have almost no marginal fitness benefit in a well-pollinated population but become strongly beneficial when local assurance falls;
2. **partitioning prediction:** a trait can improve net fitness by changing *when* or *how efficiently* interactions occur, even when total daily visitation is unchanged.

## What changes now

Immediate field priorities remain:
1. orientation first;
2. W/coloured function second where flowering overlaps;
3. validated phyllary/spine only after botanical validation.

But interpretation changes:
- do not treat visitor count as the pollination-benefit endpoint;
- estimate pollination dependence and current pollen deficit separately in a nested subset;
- where feasible, pair pollination supplementation with post-anthesis antagonist protection to put both marginal fitness effects on the same scale;
- run seed-to-recruitment microsite work only conditionally after a seed-output difference is demonstrated;
- preserve time windows because interaction partitioning can be invisible in all-day totals.

## Claim boundary

The machine-readable audit is a falsification framework over heterogeneous evidence. Counts of supporting studies are not meta-analytic weights, posterior probabilities or formal model-selection evidence. HGA3 and HGA4 are newly generated hypotheses; they remain hypotheses until the focal factorial/manipulation tests are run.
