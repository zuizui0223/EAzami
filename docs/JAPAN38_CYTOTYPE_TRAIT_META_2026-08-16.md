# Japan-38 cytotype × capitulum trait meta — 2026-08-16

## Question

Can the striking capitulum variation inside the dominant Japanese *Cirsium* radiation be reduced to a simple ploidy-state explanation before the accepted branch-length tree is available?

## Source-backed panel

A conservative existing-data panel currently contains nine Japan-38 paper concepts with explicit taxon-level cytotype evidence:

- eight concepts assigned to the dominant Japanese radiation under the frozen origin meta-synthesis;
- one secondary-arrival candidate, *C. dipsacolepis*.

The dominant-radiation panel contains:

- 2x: 5 concepts;
- 4x: 2 concepts;
- 6x: 1 concept.

This expands the earlier five-focal-taxon illustration but remains sparse relative to the full 38 concepts. Cytotypes are taxon-level records and are not assumed to be the exact Moreyra sequenced individual's state or every population's state.

## Trait overlap

Source-backed categorical orientation is available for seven of the eight dominant-radiation cytotype concepts after excluding a retained source conflict for *C. maritimum*.

Observed pattern:

- upward/ascending capitula occur in 2x, 4x and 6x dominant-radiation taxa;
- 2x dominant-radiation taxa include both upward/erect and downward/nodding capitula;
- the current secondary-arrival comparator *C. dipsacolepis* is 2x and upward/erect.

Therefore the observed panel is incompatible with a **deterministic one-to-one rule** such as `polyploid = upward` or `diploid = downward`.

This is useful because it prevents HMM3/HMM4 from collapsing into a simplistic ploidy explanation. If ploidy contributes to evolvability, it must be tested through transition history, ancestry/homeolog dynamics or diversification association rather than by assuming a fixed morphological syndrome.

## Relation to the pre-tree trait-space result

The independent public-image analysis already found that the replicated secondary-history lineage *C. lineare* is not uniquely separated in current capitulum trait space: a dominant-radiation taxon (*C. sieboldii*) has a larger leave-one-out multivariate displacement, and the largest within-dominant pairwise trait distance exceeds the largest observed *lineare*→dominant distance.

The cytotype overlap adds a second pre-tree constraint:

> large phenotype disparity inside the dominant radiation is not trivially explained by one cytotype class.

Together these results strengthen the need for a branch-length/topology-aware test of modular trait change and cytogenetic history.

## What remains public-data testable

After the accepted 294/296 topology ensemble exists:

1. reconstruct cytotype changes only where source-backed chromosome/ploidy states can be aligned to resolved taxon concepts;
2. estimate capitulum trait changes on the same tree ensemble;
3. compare whether large trait changes cluster near cytotype shifts more often than expected under sampling/age controls;
4. keep genome-size shifts separate from chromosome-number/ploidy changes;
5. compare dominant-radiation patterns with secondary histories.

## What requires new biological data

Taxon-level records cannot resolve:

- mixed cytotypes within populations;
- the exact cytotype of a RAD/target-capture individual;
- homeolog-specific ancestry in polyploids;
- whether a cytotype shift preceded or followed a focal trait transition;
- whether ploidy altered ecological performance or fitness.

Those are the reasons flow cytometry is linked to the same Phase-A RAD individuals where feasible.

## Claim boundary

The current 9-concept panel demonstrates cytotype diversity and non-deterministic observed trait correspondence only. It does not show statistical independence, estimate transition rates, establish ploidy as an evolvability driver, or demonstrate adaptive radiation.
