# Priority Japan-radiation trait recovery — 2026-08-16

## Why this recovery matters

The current Azami exhaustive public-image source pool is still sparse for the 38 Japanese paper taxon concepts used in the Moreyra 2025 radiation analysis. This creates a **trait-coverage gap**, not automatically a phylogenetic gap.

The first two recovery targets were selected before broad new sampling:

1. *Cirsium dipsacolepis* — current second secondary-arrival candidate, already represented by a Moreyra high-dimensional nuclear tip but absent from the Azami image source pool;
2. *Cirsium alpicola* — member of the dominant radiation with an existing 6x cytotype record, useful for separating trait disparity from simple ploidy or colonization-history distance.

## Authority-backed morphology recovered

Source for both taxa: National Museum of Nature and Science (Japan), `日本のアザミ` taxon database.

### *Cirsium dipsacolepis*

Authority taxon description records:

- capitula erect/upward;
- florets pale reddish-purple;
- involucre bowl-shaped to broadly campanulate, 15–20 mm in living material;
- phyllaries in 6–7 rows, coriaceous, spreading to recurved;
- outer phyllaries narrowly ovate with long caudate tips;
- glands absent and involucre non-sticky;
- habitat: temperate dry grassland;
- chromosome number: 2n=34.

This directly recovers categorical orientation, colour, involucre architecture, stickiness and cytotype context for the missing secondary-history comparator.

### *Cirsium alpicola*

Authority taxon description records:

- 2–5 capitula, erect to ascending;
- florets pale reddish-purple;
- involucre campanulate, 18–20 mm;
- phyllaries in 6–7 rows, needle-like and ascending;
- narrow-elliptic inner-phyllary glands are vestigial and the involucre is non-sticky;
- habitat: alpine grassland and mountain forest edge in southern Hokkaido/Aomori;
- chromosome number: 2n=6x=102.

## Immediate comparative implication

The two taxa share several broad display states despite very different Japanese-history roles and cytotype context:

- upward/erect head presentation;
- pale reddish-purple florets;
- non-sticky involucres.

They differ strongly in involucral architecture:

- *dipsacolepis*: hard, spreading/recurved phyllaries;
- *alpicola*: needle-like but ascending phyllaries.

This reinforces the working idea that capitulum phenotype should be treated as a **combination of modules**, not a single morphology-distance axis. A lineage can share colour/orientation with another lineage while differing strongly in defensive/involucral architecture.

This is a source-backed qualitative comparison, not a phylogenetic correlated-evolution test.

## Effect on the pre-tree radiation analysis

The quantitative Azami pre-tree trait-space analysis still cannot include *dipsacolepis* or *alpicola* because the authority descriptions are categorical/taxon-level and are not commensurate with the seven continuous image-derived endpoints used in `japan_radiation_pre_tree_trait_disparity_v1`.

Therefore:

- do **not** manufacture continuous values from the descriptions;
- do **not** mix categorical authority states into the Euclidean image-trait analysis;
- preserve them as a parallel evidence layer now;
- after the accepted nuclear tree, use them in categorical sensitivity / state-transition analyses where the ontology matches;
- continue targeted legal/public-image recovery if quantitative traits are needed.

## What is now known without new biological sampling

For *dipsacolepis*, the previous state `nuclear placement known / phenotype missing` can be refined to:

> `nuclear placement known / major taxon-level capitulum states recovered / continuous image-trait distribution still missing`.

For *alpicola*:

> `nuclear placement known / major taxon-level capitulum states + 6x cytotype recovered / continuous image-trait distribution still missing`.

## Remaining data gate

New field material is **not yet required merely to know these broad states**. Field/herbarium measurement becomes necessary when the question is quantitative population variation, proxy validation, cytotype-specific morphology or trait→fitness function.

For *dipsacolepis*, population sampling remains relevant later to compare within-Japan ancestry/reticulation/cytotype with the dominant radiation, but its species-level placement should be resolved first from the public nuclear tree.

For *alpicola*, flow-cytometric confirmation in sampled populations is required before treating the 6x taxon-level record as a population-wide state.

## Claim boundary

Authority taxon descriptions provide botanical states and useful sampling priors. They are not equivalent to individual-level measurements, morph-linked genotypes, evolutionary rates, causal adaptation or population cytotype surveys.
